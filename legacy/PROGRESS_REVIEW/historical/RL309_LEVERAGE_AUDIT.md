# RL309 leverage audit and route ranking

## Audit verdict by era

The historical programme accumulated many strong local and branch-level results. The repeated obstruction was not lack of exact mathematics but lack of a theorem with the correct global quantifiers.

### Early local obstruction era

Radius-3 and associated local geometry showed that exact small transport configurations can be impossible. The missing step was global production of the exact local hypotheses.

### Coverage / cascade era

Farey and branch-cascade work produced exact branch elimination but exposed the absence of an exhaustive branch-to-global coverage/domination theorem.

### Ownership / content / quotient era

Prime-prefix, content, quotient, and physical-state approaches sought information independent of homogeneous recurrence identities. The quotient layer succeeded in exposing genuine ordinary `+1` physical states; broad natural additive/rational residual consumers were later shown insufficient.

### H21 / incidence / charge era

Very large exact automata and charge systems provided substantial local structure. They repeatedly required a new independent global datum, incidence theorem, or lifting mechanism.

### Radius-4 / Radius-5 era

Radius-4 and Radius-5 local obstruction theorems were completed. Their global-bridge attempts established an important strategic barrier: fixed bounded local patterns do not automatically scale to arbitrary cycles. RL274 isolated scale-growing ownership/compression/replication as the missing mechanism.

### Late Gate-A / Bellman era

Fixed-seed ancestry, valuation, Bellman and P/Q work created exact reductions and strong local machinery. RL291 explicitly identified the recurring project-wide missing resource as a global physical gauge-invariant reachability/ownership constraint. Subsequent local work improved the downstream Gate-A frontier but did not remove Gate B or the common global scope deficit.

## Candidate ranking under backwards hardest-first methodology

### 1. Universal full-D ownership coercivity/extraction — PRIMARY

Why first:

- attacks the parent-nearest missing infinite arrow;
- begins from universal genuine-cycle structure;
- can potentially bypass both Gate A and Gate B;
- can consume Radius 3/4/5 and other mature machinery after producing their hypotheses;
- has strong historical evidence identifying what a successful theorem must accomplish;
- can be falsified early if a proposed invariant merely moves the same unbounded parameter elsewhere.

### 2. Direct quotient / ordinary `+1` global theorem — FALLBACK 1

Why retain:

- universal ownership-sensitive entrance exists;
- known local fake fails before quotient integrality;
- if a genuinely global non-coboundary invariant exists, it could produce direct contradiction.

Why not primary:

- RL206 already kills broad natural residual families;
- no current theorem-shaped global quotient invariant is as mature as the general scale-growing coercivity target.

### 3. Whole-cycle growth / Diophantine bypass — FALLBACK 2

Why retain:

- very short logical distance to the final theorem if successful;
- universal whole-cycle data.

Why lower:

- historical approximation/Farey routes repeatedly lacked RL-specific global restrictions or coverage.

### 4. Gate A + Gate B — FALLBACK 3

Why retain:

- largest body of exact project-specific machinery;
- many useful frozen reductions and verifiers.

Why demoted:

- at least two independent major global arrows;
- current active Gate-A frontier contains nested local/scalar obligations;
- solving current residuals does not collapse Gate B.

## Parent difficulty policy

A session is strategically successful only when its exact result makes a named parent arrow easier.

`EASIER` examples:

- arbitrary scale -> bounded scale;
- branch-specific -> exhaustive universal;
- uncontrolled context -> finite state;
- two independent global obligations -> one;
- recurrence identity -> independent physical constraint.

`LATERAL` examples:

- one more finite depth;
- one more exact residue cut without global lift;
- improved constants with the same unbounded tail;
- a new coordinate representation of the same recurrence.

`HARDER` examples:

- replacing one unbounded context with another;
- requiring an equally strong coverage theorem after the proposed theorem;
- adding a branch assumption not known for all cycles.
