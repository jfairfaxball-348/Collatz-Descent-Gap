### Central RL99 conclusion

The smallest theorem now standing between the strongest recent machinery and a global Gate-A result is a **first-surplus Farey branch lifting / domination theorem**.

The exact first-Farey pair

`(p*,q*)=(114,208,327,604, 72,057,431,991)`

is the smallest reduced ratio permitted by the RL83 product tube under the inherited external least-state floor `R>=2^71`. But the existing proof does **not** show that every hypothetical Gate-A violation must attain this smallest pair. The first surplus may occur at a larger admissible reduced denominator. RL87–RL97 then specialize to the exact first-Farey branch.

Therefore the highest-leverage RL100 question is not “how far can the four-tier frontier be scanned?” It is:

> **Can every genuine full-phase Gate-A violation be forced into the exact first-Farey branch, or can every larger-Farey branch be proved no harder than the first branch (or eliminated by a finite/exhaustive complement theorem)?**

A positive answer would convert the most successful recent computational-analytic engine from a branch sieve into a candidate global Gate-A engine. A negative answer would reveal the actual complementary branch geometry that future work must cover.

## 4. Generalized-increment (`s=1`) audit

RL79 proves that every primitive above-resonance word canonically realizes a positive primitive cycle for a generalized shortcut map `T_s`, with genuine ordinary-Collatz ownership exactly the exceptional case `s=1`.

This yields a mandatory discriminator for global routes.

### Routes that fail or weaken under the discriminator

- Pure cyclic-word grammar: unchanged under generalized increment; insufficient.
- Rotation-numerator algebra modulo `D`: rank-one/coboundary collapse; insufficient.
- Full-return monodromy prime/order analysis: ownership makes the full map the identity modulo the relevant denominator; insufficient.
- Normalized moment/Vandermonde constructions: homogeneous or pure word identities; insufficient.
- Any argument expressed only in normalized rational phases `Q_m/D`: cannot distinguish `s=1`.

### Routes that genuinely consume ordinary `+1` / physical ownership

- RL80 odd-predecessor basin rule `(2y-1)/3` and LTE blue comb: `s=1` specific, though not by itself a cycle trap.
- RL81/RL87 paired physical-state lift and difference: the common `+1` cancels during synchronized motion, but **integrality, primitive physical-state separation, and the physical maximum** are ownership inputs that generalized word normalization does not provide for free.
- RL88/RL89 reset constants such as `-1,-3,-5`: explicitly non-homogeneous ordinary-`+1` data.
- RL83 segment product/Farey tube: uses actual positive cycle states and the ordinary odd-step factor `1+1/(3y)`; it is not merely a normalized word identity.

The recent cascade therefore passes the RL79 red-team more convincingly than the earlier word-only routes. Its remaining weakness is branch coverage, not generalized-increment homogeneity.

## 5. Branch-to-global scope audit

The most important scope distinctions are:

### Global/inherited within the retained Gate-A architecture

- terminal even `k` impossibility;
- `H<=24` exact certificate and resulting `k<=25` closure;
- rank displacement and terminal valuation reductions;
- full-phase ownership framework when invoking genuine owned paired trajectories;
- synchronized physical difference divisibility for a genuine synchronized block;
- aggregate reset algebra and area bookkeeping once the relevant full-phase/physical branch hypotheses are present.

### Restricted subfamilies / laboratories

- **safe-CF fixed survivor:** historical restricted subtree; its numerical defect constants are not uniform Gate A.
- **nested `g=0`:** useful exact tail/residue laboratory, not all Gate A.
- **low-`k` RL73 giant macro:** global under its stated full-phase one-excursion hypotheses in its low-`k` range, but later physical contradiction required a maximum-scale branch.
- **exact first-Farey branch:** the key RL83–RL97 restriction. The first-surplus reduced pair is exactly `(p*,q*)`; larger admissible Farey pairs are not covered by the current cascade.
- **minimal reset:** the 2-adic rectangle certificate is a local theorem for minimal reset transitions. RL88/RL89 supply the aggregate area/exception accounting that embeds these local certificates in a broader reset family. The promoted cascade does **not** assume every reset is minimal.
- **physical representative separation:** this is a required ownership safeguard, not an optional selector; quotient/address multiplicity is never silently counted as distinct physical states.

### Smallest missing lifting theorem

The missing theorem is not “prove all first-surplus ratios equal the first Farey pair,” which may be false. The right theorem shape is broader:

> **Farey-branch domination/coverage theorem.** For every genuine full-phase Gate-A violation, let `(p,q)` be the reduced first-surplus count pair from the cycle maximum. Prove either:
> 1. `(p,q)=(p*,q*)`; or
> 2. the larger-`q` branch obeys a physical maximum/block/reset bound at least as restrictive as the first-Farey branch; or
> 3. all complementary pairs lie in a rigorously finite family that can be exhausted with explicit certificates.

This is the first theorem to attack in RL100.

Thus 3.5M is a natural **structural reconnaissance threshold**, not an expected closure threshold.

It should be preserved and resumed only after a structural trigger, not run automatically as RL100's main route.

## 7. Gate B and direct-route reassessment

Gate B has gained less concrete closure progress than Gate A since RL72. Radius 3 remains an exceptionally valuable closed local engine, but there is still no theorem forcing a genuine full-`D` object to contain the required close owned pair.

The strongest Gate-B target remains a **`D|Q`/ownership-sensitive close-pair or strict-excursion packing theorem**. Any candidate must discriminate against the RL20 radius-4 fake and must not reduce to the RL72 proper-factor/state-coboundary identities.

Direct bypass routes remain attractive because they could avoid completing both gates. But RL79 imposes a severe filter: the route must consume the absolute ordinary increment `s=1`, exact integer content, physical basin membership, or another non-homogeneous ownership fact. A normalized word invariant is not enough.

The RL80 auxiliary-basin contact remains conceptually interesting, but RL81's common-mode/physical-lift barrier prevents the auxiliary blue quotient coordinate from being identified with an actual cycle state. It is not presently ranked above the Farey-lifting route.

## 8. RL100+ ranked roadmap

### Rank 1 / RL100 primary — First-surplus Farey branch domination and global Gate-A lift

**Target theorem.** Given any genuine full-phase Gate-A violation with reduced first-surplus pair `(p,q)`, prove that the exact first-Farey branch is exhaustive or worst-case, or reduce the complement to a finite explicitly certifiable family.

**Why first.** It attacks the exact scope gap blocking the strongest modern machinery. A successful theorem upgrades many sessions of exact physical/2-adic work simultaneously. It requires little new bulk scan compute until its structural inequality is known.

**Success condition.** Produce a proved covering statement of the form:

`every Gate-A violation -> first-Farey branch OR complementary branch eliminated/majorized/finite-listed`.

A merely larger continued-fraction denominator cutoff is not success.

**Failure/pivot condition.** If the first session produces only another admissible larger Farey pair or another finite denominator floor without a uniform physical consumer, stop and pivot. Do not turn RL100 into a longer CF table.

### Rank 2 fallback — `D|Q`-sensitive Gate-B owned close-pair / strict-excursion theorem

Attack a nonlocal transport/packing inequality that fails on the RL20 `D∤Q` fake but holds on genuine owned full-phase data. Best success is to force the radius-3 input contract; weaker success is an absolute small radius bound that makes a finite local extension meaningful.

Stop if the candidate reduces to a state coboundary/proper-factor identity or survives unchanged on the RL20 fake.

### Rank 3 fallback — non-homogeneous `s=1` extremal/physical bridge

Combine the minimum/maximum asymmetry, RL83 segment product, RL80 ordinary-`+1` basin arithmetic, and RL81/RL87 physical lift to seek an equality-level or integer-content theorem that cannot exist for generalized `T_s` cycles. The theorem must explicitly use `s=1`, physical ordering, exact `+1` constants, or basin membership.

Stop if normalization removes `s` or if the result remains a pure parity-word identity.

### Rank 4 preserved fallback — resume the coupled four-tier cascade

The exact resume state is frozen in `RL98_COUPLED_CASCADE_RESUME_AFTER_RL99.md`.

Resume when at least one of the following is true:

1. RL100 proves that the first-Farey branch is exhaustive or worst-case for Gate A, so every new interval exclusion has global leverage;
2. a structural theorem yields a new tier/support/successor charge expected to remove a nontrivial fraction of the remaining branch rather than only extending the recent marginal scan rate;
3. no higher-value structural bridge survives red-team and the cascade remains the best certifiable cumulative route;
4. a focused reconnaissance at the approaching 3.5M geometric transition is needed to discover a new support regime—not merely to add another small interval.

## 10. Final answer to the RL99 central question

The first 99 sessions have not proved nontrivial Collatz cycles impossible. They have, however, moved the project from a collection of local/radius ideas to a much sharper architecture with explicit ownership tests, exact countermodels, robust finite certificates, and a strong physical/2-adic branch engine.

The strongest defensible statement is:

> **The project now knows how to eliminate a large and increasingly structured portion of one genuine full-phase Farey branch, and it knows many reasons why simpler global bridges cannot work. The principal missing theorem is no longer another local digit or another raw scan. It is a branch-to-global ownership/Farey theorem that tells us why every hypothetical Gate-A violation must enter the machinery we have built—or how to exhaust the branches that do not.**

That is why RL100 is assigned to first-surplus Farey branch domination/global lifting, with the exact 3M coupled cascade preserved as a high-quality fallback rather than discarded.
