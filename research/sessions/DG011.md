## 2. DG-011-A: sharp mod-9 shield for all expanding inverse words (PROVED)

**Theorem.** If \(b\equiv7\pmod9\) and an actual parity word w of length d with s odd steps carries a positive integer z to b with \(3^s>2^d\), then \(s\ge7\). No strict-prefix-safety assumption is needed.

**Proof.** For s=0 expansion is impossible. For s=1, expansion forces d=1, and an odd step ends 2 mod3, not 7 mod9. Let \(s\ge2\), let r be the terminal number of zeros, and let g be the index distance between the last two odd symbols. Using the affine recursion \(q\mapsto3q+2^i\) on the odd step at zero-based position i, only the *last two* odd contributions survive modulo9:
\[
q_w\equiv2^{d-r-1}+3\cdot2^{d-r-g-1}\pmod9.
\]
Since 9 divides \(3^s z\),
\[
b\equiv2^{-(r+1)}(1+3\cdot2^{-g})\equiv
\begin{cases}
4\cdot2^{-(r+1)}\pmod9&g\text{ even},\\
7\cdot2^{-(r+1)}\pmod9&g\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
Because 2 has order 6 modulo9, \(b\equiv7\pmod9\) demands either (i) odd g and \(r\equiv5\pmod6\), forcing \(d\ge s+5\), or (ii) even g and \(r\equiv3\pmod6\), with at least one zero between the last two ones, forcing \(d\ge s+4\). But for \(s\le6\), \((3/2)^s\le729/64<16\). In both cases \(2^d\ge2^{s+4}>3^s\), contrary to expansion. So \(s\ge7\). \(\square\)

**Sharp example.** The word \(w=11111101000\) has \((3^s,q_w,2^d)=(2187,2123,2048)\), \(s=7\), and the true trajectory
\[
191\to287\to431\to647\to971\to1457\to2186\to1093\to1640\to820\to410\to205.
\]
Here \(205\equiv7\bmod9\), every interior point exceeds 205, and the word is strict-prefix-safe. Thus seven is achievable. No E membership is inferred for 191 or 205.

**Scope:** This bans *expanding words with s≤6*, including all such strict-safe words, not nonexpanding inverse words or all deeper s≥7 roots. It is stronger than DG009's enumeration for two particular nine-gap progressions because it applies to **every** 7-mod9 endpoint with no prefix-safety assumption.
