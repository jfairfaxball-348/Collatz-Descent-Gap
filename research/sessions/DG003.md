# DG003 — exact cross-depth collapse and least-exception inverse sieve (2026-10-15)

**Status: EXECUTED.** Elementary paper proofs plus finite independent Python checks. The central statement \(E=\varnothing\) remains OPEN. These are programme-level mathematical restrictions, **not** an assertion of novel research, a Lean/Palomar verification, or a Collatz solution. Next: [DG004](../NEXT_SESSION_DG004.md).

## 0. Problem and notation

Let \(T(x)=x/2\) if \(x\) is even, and \(T(x)=(3x+1)/2\) if \(x\) is odd. Let
\[
E=\{n\ge2:\ T^j(n)\ge n\text{ for all }j\ge1\}.
\]
By Terras–Everett, \(E\) has natural density zero. Whether it is empty is open.

For a parity word \(v\) of length \(L\), write \(B_v=3^{s(v)}\), \(H_v=2^L\), and \(Q_v\ge0\) so that its specified affine branch is
\[
T^L(z)=\frac{B_vz+Q_v}{H_v}.
\]
DG001 establishes a one-to-one correspondence between length-\(L\) parity words and residue classes modulo \(2^L\). DG002 establishes the exact repeated-block congruence.

## 1. DG-003-A — shifted exact congruence contains no hidden second constraint (PROVED)

**Theorem (cylinder-equivalence of cross-depth block congruence).**
Fix an arbitrary prefix word \(u\) of length \(J\), and a nonempty word \(w\) of length \(m\). Put
\[
(B,2^J,Q)=(3^{s(u)},2^J,q_u),\quad (A,D,q)=(3^{s(w)},2^m,q_w).
\]
For any integer \(t\ge1\), let \(K=J+mt\). Then an ordinary positive integer \(n\) has **exactly** the specified initial parity word \(uw^t\) if and only if
\[
\boxed{2^{J+mt}\mid (D-A)(Bn+Q)-2^Jq.}\tag{1}
\]
In particular this is a *single* parity-cylinder congruence, not an independent arithmetic consequence that can intersect the same cylinder more thinly.

**Proof.** If \(n\) has initial word \(u\), then \(x=T^J(n)=(Bn+Q)/2^J\). If it next follows \(w^t\), DG002 gives \(D^t\mid (D-A)x-q\). Multiplying by \(2^J\) gives (1).

For the converse, the coefficient of \(n\) in (1) is \((D-A)B\), which is odd and hence invertible modulo \(2^K\). Therefore (1) selects exactly one residue class modulo \(2^K\). By DG001 there exists exactly one parity-cylinder residue class for \(uw^t\), and its members satisfy (1) by the forward direction. The classes therefore coincide. QED.

For example \(u=10,\ w=110,\ t=2\) yields \(J=2,B=3,Q=1,D-A=-1,q=5\), so (1) is \(256\mid -3n-21\), or \(n\equiv249\pmod{256}\); the unique parity word of that class is \(10(110)^2\). **Implication killed:** separate shifted congruences for compatible blocks cannot, by themselves, make the positive representatives of a parity word disappear. Any new cross-depth bridge needs an additional non-cylindrical property of a *fixed positive integer*, not a second encoding of its parity bits.

## 2. DG-003-B — safe backward predecessor transfer and an infinite least-exception sieve (PROVED)

**Lemma (safe-ancestor transfer).** Suppose \(y\in E\), \(2\le z<y\), and for some \(L\ge1\),
\[
T^L(z)=y,\qquad T^j(z)\ge z\quad(1\le j<L).
\]
Then \(z\in E\).

**Proof.** For the first \(L-1\) steps this is assumed, and at step \(L\), \(y>z\). For every \(k\ge L\), \(T^k(z)=T^{k-L}(y)\ge y>z\), because \(y\in E\). Thus every iterate of \(z\) is at least \(z\). QED.

This transfer is deliberately **backward and conditional on the entire finite bridge staying above \(z\)**. It does *not* assert that forward iterates of an exception are exceptional, nor that arbitrary inverse preimages are exceptional.

**Lemma (strictly expanding word yields safe bridge).** If a parity word \(w\) satisfies \(3^{s_j}>2^j\) for all \(1\le j\le |w|\), then every positive \(z\) realising \(w\) satisfies \(T^j(z)>z\) for those steps. Indeed DG001 gives \(T^j(z)=(3^{s_j}z+q_j)/2^j>z\), since \(q_j\ge0\). This suffices to verify the safe-ancestor condition for a word that ends at \(y>z\).

**Theorem (three disjoint infinite forbidden classes for \(\min E\)).** If \(E\ne\varnothing\) and \(m=\min E\), then
\[
\boxed{m\not\equiv5\pmod6,\qquad m\not\equiv13\pmod{18},\qquad m\not\equiv91\pmod{162}.}\tag{2}
\]
The three classes are pairwise disjoint among odd integers; exactly \(37\) of the \(81\) odd residues modulo \(162\) lie in their union. This is a restriction on the **single least hypothetical exception**; it is NOT a natural-density bound or a prohibition on all members of \(E\).

**Constructive proof.** In each row \(h\ge0\); the displayed smaller integer \(z\ge2\) runs along the specified parity word, never dips below \(z\), and ends at \(y\).

| End \(y\) | Earlier \(z<y\) | Word from \(z\) | Exact affine relation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(5+6h\) | \(3+4h\) | \(1\) | \(y=(3z+1)/2\) |
| \(13+18h\) | \(11+16h\) | \(110\) | \(y=(9z+5)/8\) |
| \(91+162h\) | \(71+128h\) | \(111010\) | \(y=(81z+73)/64\) |

All three words have \(3^{s_j}>2^j\) at every nonempty prefix:
- \(1\): \(3>2\).
- \(110\): \(3>2,\ 9>4,\ 9>8\).
- \(111010\): \(3>2,\ 9>4,\ 27>8,\ 27>16,\ 81>32,\ 81>64\).

For complete transparency, the actual third-row finite segment is
\[
71+128h\to107+192h\to161+288h\to242+432h
\to121+216h\to182+324h\to91+162h.
\]
It is everywhere above the starting \(71+128h\), including at the endpoint. The other rows are
\[
3+4h\to5+6h
\quad\text{and}\quad
11+16h\to17+24h\to26+36h\to13+18h.
\]
In every case \(z<y\). Were \(y=m\in E\), the safe-ancestor lemma would give a strictly smaller member \(z\in E\), contrary to minimality. Therefore (2). The residue counts modulo \(162\) are \(27\) from \(5\bmod6\), \(9\) from \(13\bmod18\), and \(1\) from \(91\bmod162\), with no overlap. QED.

**Scope and significance.** Unlike DG002's path-realisation obstructions, this is an infinite-horizon necessary arithmetic condition on the *least actual positive-integer survivor*, derived from fully specified finite inverse bridges. It does **not** show that the corresponding residue classes are disjoint from \(E\): other, nonleast exceptions may hypothetically occupy them. It is elementary and not claimed as new to the literature.

## 3. DG-003-C — nonperiodic exceptions force infinitely many exceptions (PROVED)

**Theorem (suffix-minimum exceptional set).** If some \(n\in E\) has an unbounded forward \(T\)-orbit \(x_j=T^j(n)\), then \(E\) contains infinitely many distinct members of that same orbit, tending to infinity. More precisely, for every \(J\ge0\) let
\[
a_J=\min_{j\ge J}x_j,
\]
and pick a time \(i_J\ge J\) with \(x_{i_J}=a_J\). Then \(a_J\in E\) and \(a_J\to\infty\).

**Proof.** The tail minimum exists by well-ordering, because all orbit values are positive. It is attained at \(i_J\), and every later term is at least \(a_J\), so \(a_J\in E\) (all values are at least the original \(n\ge2\)). Were the orbit to repeat a value, deterministic iteration would enter a finite cycle and be bounded; hence an unbounded orbit has pairwise distinct \(x_j\). For each \(M\), at most \(M\) distinct values can be in \([1,M]\), so beyond some index all \(x_j>M\). Consequently \(a_J>M\) for large \(J\), proving divergence and infinitude. QED.

**Corollary (precise exceptional-set dichotomy).** If \(E\ne\varnothing\), either there exists a nontrivial positive periodic Collatz orbit (a cycle with values above \(1\)), or \(E\) is infinite and unbounded. Indeed an \(E\)-orbit that is bounded eventually repeats and becomes a nontrivial cycle; otherwise apply the theorem. **Crucial limitation:** an infinite unbounded subset can have natural density zero. No positive-density lower bound follows from the argument, and excluding cycles alone would not prove \(E=\varnothing\).

## 4. Exact obstruction to extending the inverse sieve: multiples of three (PROVED)

For \(y\equiv0\pmod3\), every one-step positive integer predecessor \(z\) with \(T(z)=y\) must be even, and is necessarily \(z=2y\). Indeed for odd \(z\), \(T(z)=(3z+1)/2\equiv2\pmod3\). Repeating backward from a multiple of three therefore produces **only** \(2^ky\), all strictly greater than \(y\). Thus no smaller safe backward ancestor of *any* length exists for an \(y\equiv0\pmod3\). The least-exception inverse method, unaided, cannot rule out \(m\equiv0\pmod3\). This is a mathematical barrier, not evidence that such exceptions exist.

## 5. Mandatory attacks and falsification ledger

1. **Shifted expanding-block congruences at separated depths:** Derived exact (1) and proved its equivalence to the ordinary cylinder. Nested compatible congruences convey no independent integer elimination. Varying nonperiodic blocks does not fix this logical deficiency.
2. **Least exception:** A naive claim "\(T^j(m)\in E\)" is not proved and was not used. Instead, the safe-ancestor lemma provides an actual direction of propagation with an explicit finite check; (2) excludes three disjoint infinite arithmetic classes for the *minimum* only.
3. **Entire numerical subclasses of \(E\):** Attempted to promote the inverse sieve to \(E\cap\{\text{those classes}\}=\varnothing\). **Not proved**: safe ancestors of nonleast \(E\)-members may simply be other members of \(E\). A numerical-family exclusion valid for all \(E\), beyond already-known finite-prefix descent certificates, is still outstanding.
4. **All odd survivors have odd prefix majority:** DG002's \(s_j>j/2\) remains necessary but does not force an inverse ancestor, an upper bound on its starting value, or an incompatible lower density. The three safe paths are consistent with this condition.
5. **Finite cylinders and integer realisation:** DG001's \(2^kt-1\) survives \(k\) odd steps, and DG002's \(8^t-5\) survives \(3t\) mixed steps. Their initial words and the nonperiodic 2-adic positive-integer-free word from DG002 remain counterexamples to upgrading finite cylinder information to a fixed infinite positive orbit.
6. **Infinitude-to-positive-density:** The suffix-minimum theorem yields infinitely many exceptions conditional on an unbounded exceptional orbit, not a uniform count in \([1,X]\); a zero-density infinite set is consistent with both it and Terras–Everett. Positive density, let alone a contradiction, has not been established.
