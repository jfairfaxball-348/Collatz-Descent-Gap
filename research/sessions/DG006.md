## 1. Exact bridge geometry — DG-006-A (PROVED)

Suppose \(n\in E\) has an unbounded orbit, with successive distinct, strictly increasing suffix minima
\(a=x_{t_j}<b=x_{t_{j+1}}\), both in \(E\), and gap \(d=t_{j+1}-t_j\).
DG004/005 prove \(a,b\equiv3\pmod4\), \(b\le T(a)\), \(d\ne2\), and, if \(d\ge3\),
the final step is \(2b\to b\). Hence any long bridge has first two parity bits \(11\)
(because \(a\equiv3\pmod4\)), final bit \(0\), and all intermediate values **strictly exceed** \(b\).
The strictness follows because an unbounded orbit has no repeated values.
The intermediate-inequality condition refers to this particular finite segment; it is necessary, not sufficient, for its endpoints to be in \(E\).

For any length-\(d\) parity word \(w\), write its prefix branches
\(x_i=(A_i a+q_i)/2^i\), where \(A_i=3^{s_i}\), \(q_0=0\), and
\(q_{i+1}=3q_i+2^i\) for bit \(1\), \(q_{i+1}=q_i\) for bit \(0\).
Set \(A=A_d\), \(q=q_d\), \(D=2^d\).
The **complete finite arithmetic/inequality test** for a potential long suffix-minimum bridge is:

1. \(w\) starts \(11\) and ends \(0\); \(a\ge3\);
2. \(a\equiv A^{-1}(3D-q)\pmod{4D}\), so \(b=(Aa+q)/D\) is an integer \(3\pmod4\)
   and (by parity-cylinder uniqueness) \(a\) actually realises word \(w\);
3. \((A-D)a+q>0\), equivalent to \(b>a\);
4. \((2^{d-i}A_i-A)a>q-2^{d-i}q_i\) for each \(1\le i<d\), equivalent to \(x_i>b\).

These conditions are **necessary for every actual exceptional-record bridge** and sufficient
only for the specified **finite local shape**. In particular they do *not*
prove that \(a\) or \(b\) belongs to \(E\), or that \(b\) is a minimum of the infinite tail.

### Exact classification at gaps 3, 4, 5 and 6

- \(d=2\): impossible (the first two bits are \(11\), but the required penultimate even input to \(b\) would force bit \(1=0\)).
- \(d=3\): the only word is \(110\). Necessarily and sufficiently for the finite local bridge,
  \[
  a=27+32h,\quad b=(9a+5)/8=31+36h,\quad h\ge0.
  \]
  Indeed \(b\equiv3\pmod4\) forces \(a\equiv27\pmod{32}\);
  \(x_1-b=(3a-1)/8>0\), \(x_2=2b\), and \(b-a=(a+5)/8>0\).
  **Every genuine gap-3 target is therefore \(1\pmod3\)**.
- \(d=4\): candidate words \(1100\), \(1110\).
  Their endpoints are \((9a+5)/16\) and \((27a+19)/16\).
  The former is below \(a\) for all \(a\ge3\);
  the latter exceeds \(x_1=(3a+1)/2\) by \((3a+11)/16\).
  Neither is a valid bridge.
- \(d=5\): candidate words \(11000,11010,11100,11110\), with endpoints
  \((9a+5)/32,(27a+23)/32,(27a+19)/32,(81a+65)/32\).
  The first is below \(a\).
  The middle two can exceed \(a\) only at \(a=3\), where neither endpoint is an integer
  (equivalently the prescribed parity word is not realised).
  The last exceeds \(x_1\) by \((33a+49)/32\).
  Thus **gap 5 is impossible**.
- \(d=6\): among the eight words of the form \(11***0\), the only feasible finite
  local shapes are:
  
  | Word | Source \(a\) | Target \(b\) | \(b\bmod3\) |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | \(111010\) | \(71+256h\) | \(91+324h\) | 1 |
  | \(111100\) | \(207+256h\) | \(263+324h\) | 2 |
  
  for every \(h\ge0\). Both are **actual finite positive-integer Collatz segments**
  with all intermediate terms above \(b>a\), not known \(E\)-orbits.
  The six others fail \(b>a\), \(b\le x_1\), or the intermediate lower bound:
  \(110000,110010,110100,111000\) have \(b<a\) for \(a\ge3\);
  \(110110\) has \(b>x_3\);
  \(111110\) has \(b>x_1\).
  Congruence \(b\equiv3\pmod4\) then gives the displayed unique source class
  modulo \(256=4\cdot2^6\).

**Uniform consequence:** every genuine long gap obeys the displayed parity-word,
\(4\cdot2^d\)-congruence and linear inequalities. The smallest allowed gaps
are \(d=3\) and \(d=6\); **none of \(2,4,5\) occurs**. There are no claims here
that the list of *all* larger gaps has been classified.

## 2. Mod-3 gate and finite all-1-mod-3 stress test — DG-006-B (PROVED)

For any actual parity word ending at a later record \(b\), let \(\ell\)
be the number of even steps *after its last odd step*. Every odd step outputs \(2\bmod3\);
each subsequent even step multiplies by \(2^{-1}\equiv2\pmod3\).
Consequently
\[
b\equiv 2(-1)^\ell\pmod3.
\]
At a long gap, \(\ell\ge1\). Thus \(b\equiv1\pmod3\) exactly when
\(\ell\) is odd, and \(b\equiv2\pmod3\) exactly when \(\ell\) is even.
This applies irrespective of whether the earlier source was divisible by three.
It does **not** force any parity frequency.

More strongly, for each integer \(r\ge1\), set
\[
n_r=12\cdot8^r-5,\qquad
a_j=12\cdot9^j 8^{r-j}-5,\quad 0\le j\le r.
\]
The *actual positive-integer* orbit of \(n_r\), up to time \(3r\), has
\(T^{3j}(n_r)=a_j\), with consecutive finite blocks \(110\).
All \(a_j\) are \(3\bmod4\), **all are \(1\bmod3\)**, they increase strictly,
and each intermediate value within a block exceeds its endpoint \(a_{j+1}\).
Thus these are arbitrarily long *finite* lists satisfying the genuine short
record-bridge equations, every target \(1\bmod3\). Example \(r=2\):
\[
763\to1145\to1718\to859\to1289\to1934\to967,
\]
with candidate records \(763<859<967\), all \(1\bmod3\).
They are **finite suffix minima of this truncated segment only**; subsequent
terms may and do invalidate them as infinite-orbit exceptional suffix minima.
They refute any proposed *uniform finite-block exclusion* of the all-1-mod-3 scenario.

Conversely, no fixed positive integer can follow \(110\) forever (including
eventually): each complete block maps \(x+5\mapsto9(x+5)/8\);
after \(r\) blocks, integrality requires \(8^r\mid(x+5)\).
For all \(r\) this forces \(x=-5\), not a positive integer.
Hence a genuine exceptional-record chain cannot have **eventually all** gaps \(3\).
Combined with the gap exclusions, an unbounded \(E\)-orbit has
either infinitely many gap-1 transitions or infinitely many gaps \(\ge6\).
This is a necessary dichotomy, **not** exclusion of mixed, nonperiodic
gap-1 / gap-3 schedules or of all-1-mod-3 targets at other gap lengths.

The first transition from \(m=\min E\) has an extra least-element gate:
if it is long and its target \(b\equiv2\pmod3\), the odd inverse
\(z=(2b-1)/3\) lies in \(E\) and satisfies
\(z<a_0=m\) (as \(b<T(m)\)), a contradiction.
Thus the **first** long record transition of a hypothetical unbounded orbit
starting at \(m\) must have \(b\equiv1\pmod3\).
In particular the \(111100\) finite bridge cannot serve as that first transition.
For gap 1, the target is \(2\bmod3\) and its odd inverse is just \(m\);
there is no contradiction. This does not constrain every later long target.

## 3. Disjoint multi-generation inverse branches — DG-006-C (PROVED, CONDITIONAL)

Fix an unbounded orbit from \(n\in E\), its increasing exceptional records
\(a_j\), and a long-gap target \(b=a_{j+1}\equiv2\pmod3\).
Write \(r=v_3(b+1)\ge1\) and set
\[
z_i=\frac{2^i(b+1)}{3^i}-1\qquad(1\le i\le r),\qquad z_0=b.
\]
All \(z_i\) are odd integers at least \(3\), strictly decrease in \(i\),
satisfy \(T(z_i)=z_{i-1}\), and obey \(z_i<a_j<b\).
Proof: \(3^r\mid(b+1)\); the integer \((b+1)/3^r\) is even because \(b\) is odd,
so \(z_r\ge2^{r+1}-1\ge3\); each reverse odd step shrinks
\(z_i+1\) by \(2/3\). As \(b\in E\), repeated safe-ancestor transfer
(or direct growth of the finite bridge) gives \(z_i\in E\).
Finally, \(b<T(a_j)=(3a_j+1)/2\) for a long gap, so
\(z_1=(2b-1)/3<a_j\), and \(z_i\le z_1\).

These roots lie **outside the original orbit**, since its unique predecessor
immediately before \(b\) is \(2b\), not \(z_1\). If any \(z_i\) lay on that
injective orbit, its forward path would pass through the off-spine \(z_1\)
immediately before hitting \(b\), a contradiction.
Moreover, chains for different long-gap \(2\bmod3\) record targets are pairwise
disjoint: each inverse-chain point has the *unique first intersection* with
the original orbit at its respective target \(b\), and deterministic forward
iteration cannot have two different first intersections.

With \(R(X)=\#\{j:a_j\le X\}\), this sharpens DG005 to
\[
\boxed{|E\cap[2,X]|\ \ge R(X)+
  \sum_{\substack{j:a_{j+1}\le X\\d_j\ge3,\ a_{j+1}\equiv2\ (3)}}
    v_3(a_{j+1}+1),\quad X\ge n.}
\]
It is stronger than \(R(X)+Q(X)\) whenever a target has \(v_3(b+1)>1\);
however there is **no proven lower bound** for the number of such targets.
There is not even a proof that one long-gap \(2\bmod3\) record must occur.
No density contradiction follows.

Concrete *finite* local stress tests:
- \(207\to311\to467\to701\to1052\to526\to263\) is word \(111100\)
  and has \(263\equiv2\pmod3\); its odd inverse is \(175<207\).
- \(2815\) follows word \(11111111010000\) (14 steps) to \(3383\),
  with all intervening values above \(3383\). Here
  \(v_3(3384)=2\) and \(3383\leftarrow2255\leftarrow1503\) are
  two smaller odd inverse links, with \(1503<2255<2815\).
Neither example is a known exception or a verified infinite tail minimum.

The safe inverse *forest* outside the orbit can contain further predecessors,
but a larger even inverse \(2y\) is not automatically exceptional and a
multi-edge return below its starting value needs a full safe-prefix proof.
The disjointness result applies to certified safe branches whose **first
intersection** with the orbit is their assigned record; indiscriminately
counting all inverse edges is invalid.

## 4. Positive-integer realisation gate, killed implications and remaining gap

- **Killed:** gaps 2, 4 or 5 as genuine exceptional tail-record transitions.
- **Killed:** every long-gap target must be \(1\bmod3\);
  the length-6 word \(111100\) has bona fide positive-integer local
  bridges with target \(2\bmod3\). No exceptional integer is asserted.
- **Killed:** finite congruence/bridge restrictions exclude arbitrarily long
  all-1-mod-3 schedules; the family \(12\cdot8^r-5\) provides counterexamples
  at every finite length \(r\).
- **Proved but limited:** the *eventually constant* gap-3 schedule is impossible
  for a positive seed; this is a fixed expanding-block/2-adic realisation
  obstruction, not a proof excluding every nonperiodic all-1-mod-3 schedule.
- **Proved but limited:** distinct, safe odd inverse chains below different
  long-gap \(2\bmod3\) records are disjoint and yield the weighted count
  above; no branch prevalence or positive-density conclusion follows.
- **Not justified:** arbitrary numerical record sequences can be realised
  by actual Collatz bridges; a finite local bridge can be upgraded to
  an infinite suffix-minimum/exceptional endpoint; a cycle-only argument
  establishes \(E=\varnothing\).
