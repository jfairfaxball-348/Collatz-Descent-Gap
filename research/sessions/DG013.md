# DG013 — Oscillating paired mountain obstruction; periodic-island inequality

**EXECUTED 16 October 2026.** The unique global target remains \(E=\varnothing\) for the shortened Collatz map \(T(2x)=x,\ T(2x+1)=3x+2\). **E=empty is NOT proved.** This session proves a fixed-positive-orbit theorem covering oscillatory mountain itineraries not covered by DG012, including the specified logarithmic staircase with small mountains inserted at every second bridge. No theorem is asserted for arbitrary exceptional trajectories, arbitrary oscillatory mountain tails, or bounded/preperiodic exceptional orbits.

## 1. Hypotheses, exact two-word affine arithmetic

For \(r\ge5,\ r\equiv5\pmod6\), set \(s(r)=\min\{s\ge1:(3/2)^s>2^r\}\) and \(w_r=1^{s(r)}0^r\). Each mountain endpoint on its genuine parity cylinder is
\[
F_r(x)={A_rx+q_r\over D_r},\quad A_r=3^{s(r)},\
D_r=2^{s(r)+r},\quad q_r=3^{s(r)}-2^{s(r)}.
\]
Thus \(1<A_r/D_r<3/2\), \(0<q_r/D_r<3/2\), and \(s(r)>r\). A *strict bridge* \(a\to b\) means \(a<b<T^i(a)\) for every \(0<i<|w_r|\), with exactly the word \(w_r\). Every finite list of such words admits infinitely many genuine positive strict bridge realisations with initial \(n\equiv0\pmod3\) (DG011); this is **not** infinite fixed-seed realisation.

Let \(P_R=w_5w_R\), \(R\ge5,\ R\equiv5\bmod6\). Its composite rational affine map
\[
G_R(x)={A x+Q\over D},\quad
A=3^{9+s(R)},\quad D=2^{14+s(R)+R},\quad
Q=3^{s(R)}(3^9-2^9)+2^{14}(3^{s(R)}-2^{s(R)})
\tag{1}
\]
has \(D<A\), \(Q>0\), \(C:=A-D>0\), and
\[
0<C+Q<5D. \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the composition has slope \(\lambda_5\lambda_R\in(1,9/4)\), so \(C/D<5/4\). Its intercept divided by D is \(\lambda_R(q_5/D_5)+q_R/D_R< (3/2)^2+3/2=15/4\), giving (2).

**Exact paired-repeat lemma (PROVED).** If an ordinary positive integer \(x\) actually traverses \(K\ge1\) consecutive copies of \(P_R\), then
\[
D^K\mid Cx+Q,\qquad D^{K-1}<5(x+1). \tag{3}
\]
Proof: writing \(y=G_R^K(x)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\),
\[
Cy+Q=(A/D)^K(Cx+Q).
\]
The left side is an integer and \(\gcd(A,D)=1\). Hence the divisibility. As \(Cx+Q>0\) is a multiple of \(D^K\) and \(Cx+Q<(C+Q)(x+1)<5D(x+1)\), (3) follows. The modular condition is **not an independent restriction beyond the exact concatenated parity cylinder**; the new use is that a fixed positive seed must obey the real-size inequality for arbitrarily distant blocks.

## 2. Generic periodic-island bound (PROVED)

**Lemma.** Let \(V\) be a fixed concatenation of \(m\ge1\) mountain words, of total bit length \(d(V)\). Suppose an ordinary positive \(n\) follows strict mountain bridges and, beginning at bridge index \(j\), it traverses \(K\) consecutive copies of \(V\). Then
\[
2^{(K-1)d(V)}
 <
 4\big((3/2)^m-1\big)\,2^j(n+1). \tag{4}
\]

**Proof.** Write the affine map of \(V\) as \(G(x)=(Ax+Q)/D\) with \(D=2^{d(V)}\), \(A=3^S\), \(Q>0\), \(A>D\). Each mountain has slope in (1,3/2), intercept in (0,3/2). By affine composition, \(\Lambda=A/D<(3/2)^m\) and
\(\eta=Q/D<\frac32\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}(\frac32)^h=3((3/2)^m-1)\).
Thus \((A-D+Q)/D<4((3/2)^m-1)\). The repeated-word identity proves \(D^K\mid(A-D)x+Q\) for the positive source \(x=a_j\) of this repeated segment; positivity gives \(D^{K-1}<4((3/2)^m-1)(x+1)\). Each earlier *strict* mountain bridge satisfies \(a_{i+1}<T(a_i)=(3a_i+1)/2\), hence \(a_{i+1}+1<2(a_i+1)\), so \(x+1<2^j(n+1)\) for \(j>0\) (and \(\le\) suffices if \(j=0\)). Combine. \(\square\)

The lemma applies to arbitrary *finite superwords*, not only runs of equal \(r\). It rules out a periodic island whenever its denominator exponent outgrows its allowed initial-size budget. It does not say that all infinite sequences contain such islands.

## 3. DG-013-A: fixed-small / monotone-large oscillation theorem (PROVED)

**Theorem.** Fix \(r_*\ge5,\ r_*\equiv5\bmod6\). There is no positive integer whose actual shortened Collatz orbit contains an infinite suffix of consecutive strict mountain bridges with
\[
r_{2j}=r_*,\qquad r_{2j+1}=R_j,
\]
where every \(R_j\ge5,\ R_j\equiv5\bmod6\), and \(R_j\) is *eventually nondecreasing*. This holds whether the \(R_j\) eventually stabilise or are unbounded; it allows arbitrarily large drops in the full sequence \(r_i\) from each \(R_j\) back to \(r_*\).

**Proof.** Delete a finite initial collection of complete pairs until \(R_j\) is nondecreasing, reset the first pair's source to \(n>0\), and index pairs from zero. The paired-repeat lemma (3) remains true with \(w_5\) replaced by fixed \(w_{r_*}\): both mountain slopes/intercepts have the same bounds, so \(C+Q<5D\) and \(d(R)=d(r_*)+s(R)+R>2R\).

If \(R_j\) eventually stabilises, there are arbitrarily many copies of one fixed paired superword \(w_{r_*}w_R\) starting at one fixed positive source. Inequality (3), with \(D>1\), forbids this.

Otherwise enumerate the distinct plateau values as \(\rho_k\) (indexed \(k=0,1,\dots\)), and let the \(k\)-th plateau begin at *pair index* \(J_k\), end at \(J_{k+1}\), and have \(K_k=J_{k+1}-J_k\ge1\) copies of \(w_{r_*}w_{\rho_k}\). Monotonicity and the congruence constraint give \(\rho_k\ge5+6k\). Let \(H=\log_2(n+1)\) and \(c=H+\log_2 5\).

At the beginning of the high mountain of plateau \(k\), its source is \(a_{2J_k+1}\). Its \(s(\rho_k)\) initial odd steps imply \(2^{s(\rho_k)}\mid a_{2J_k+1}+1\). The earlier strict bridge upper bounds give
\[
2^{\rho_k}<2^{s(\rho_k)}
 \le a_{2J_k+1}+1<2^{2J_k+1}(n+1),
\]
hence
\[
 J_k>{\rho_k-1-H\over2}\ge3k+{4-H\over2},
 \qquad \liminf_{k\to\infty}{J_k\over k}\ge3. \tag{5}
\]
On the other hand, applying (3) to the \(K_k\) copies of the paired word, starting at \(a_{2J_k}\), gives
\[
2^{(K_k-1)d(\rho_k)}<5(a_{2J_k}+1)
 \le5\,2^{2J_k}(n+1),
\]
where \(d(\rho_k)=d(r_*)+s(\rho_k)+\rho_k>2\rho_k\). Thus
\[
K_k<1+{2J_k+c\over 2\rho_k}
 \le1+{2J_k+c\over10+12k},\qquad
J_{k+1}\le J_k+1+{2J_k+c\over10+12k}. \tag{6}
\]
The recurrence forces \(J_k=O(k)\). Explicitly choose \(B\ge\max\{J_1,(22+c)/18\}\). If \(J_k\le B(k+1)\) for \(k\ge1\), then \((k+1)/(10+12k)\le2/22\), so
\(K_k<1+(4B+c)/22\le B\); hence \(J_{k+1}\le B(k+2)\). Induction starts at \(k=1\).

Set \(L=\limsup J_k/k<\infty\). Equation (6) gives \(\limsup K_k\le1+L/6\), while \(J_k=\sum_{i<k}K_i\) implies \(L\le\limsup K_k\). Therefore \(L\le1+L/6\), i.e. \(L\le6/5\), contradicting (5). \(\square\)

**Scope boundary:** This proves absence of a single *ordinary positive* seed for this infinite oscillatory tail. It does **not** prove that genuine arbitrary \(E\)-records have the shape \(w_{r_*}w_{R_j}\), nor exclude every oscillatory mountain sequence. All finite prefixes are realisable by infinitely many positive integers, and a consistent infinite 2-adic parity stream is not a positive-integer witness.

## 4. Explicit logarithmic oscillation and adversarial checks

For the named DG013 stress-test set
\[
r_{2j}=5,\qquad r_{2j+1}=5+6\lfloor\log_2(j+2)\rfloor.
\tag{7}
\]
On plateau \(k\ge1\), the high parameter is \(\rho_k=5+6k\), starting at \(J_k=2^k-2\) and staying constant for \(K_k=2^k\) paired words. Equation (3) plus the coarse bridge-size bound would force for the same fixed n
\[
\log_2(5(n+1))>
(2^k-1)(5+6k)-2(2^k-2)\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
which is impossible. Here the parameter \(r_j\) oscillates persistently, so DG012's eventual-nondecreasing theorem does not apply; DG013's paired-repeat argument does. The much weaker DG012 odd-run slope test does not independently kill (7), since \(r_j=O(\log j)\) at the high indices.

## 5. Actual-E bridge gap and bounded case

An actual unbounded exceptional orbit may have consecutive strict suffix-record intervals with unrestricted parity words and unrestricted lengths, not necessarily any \(w_r\). The *universal* strict-bridge inequality \(a_{j+1}+1<2(a_j+1)\) is too weak by itself to contradict \(|E\cap[1,X]|=o(X)\). DG010's off-spine first-meeting lemma requires genuinely divergent roots and a length comparison, neither forced for all arbitrary bridges; counting every finite inverse root as distinct remains invalid. DG011's sharp mod-9 shield also blocks some short-window root arguments. Thus no record-wide divergent-root recurrence or density contradiction was proved.

If a least hypothetical \(E\) seed is a multiple of 3 and preperiodic into an unknown nontrivial positive cycle above it, the cycle itself has no divisible-by-3 member (DG010), but this does not exclude the smaller preperiodic seed. The paired-repeat proof assumes an infinite *strictly increasing mountain record chain*; a bounded eventually periodic orbit has none. This case remains explicitly OPEN.
