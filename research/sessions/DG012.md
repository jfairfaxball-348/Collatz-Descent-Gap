## 1. Exact arithmetic and distinguishing an independent constraint from a cylinder

For \(r\ge5,\ r\equiv5\pmod6\), define \(s=s(r)=\min\{s\ge1:(3/2)^s>2^r\}\), \(d=s+r\), \(w_r=1^s0^r\) and
\[
F_r(a)=\frac{Aa+q}{D},\quad A=3^s,\quad D=2^{s+r},\quad q=3^s-2^s,\quad
1<\lambda=A/D<3/2.
\]
The word is strict-prefix expanding, and sufficiently large correct parity-cylinder starts realise a finite strict bridge \(a<F_r(a)<T^i(a)\) for \(0<i<d\) (DG010/DG011). In particular every bridge source is odd.

An actual consecutive run of \(s\) odd steps requires
\[
a+1=2^s k,\qquad T^s(a)=3^s k-1,\qquad 3^s k\equiv1\pmod{2^r}.
\]
If the next mountain starts with \(s'\) odd steps at \(b=F_r(a)\), then
\[
2^{s'}\mid b+1
\iff 3^s k\equiv1-2^r\pmod{2^{r+s'}}. \tag{1}
\]
Equation (1) is exactly the *concatenated parity cylinder*, not an independent arithmetic obstruction. Likewise \(F_r(a)\equiv-2^{-r}\pmod{3^k}\) whenever \(s\ge k\). The target class \(7\bmod9\) and its finite 3-adic lifts cannot themselves force any recurrence: DG011 gives arbitrary finite realisations.

## 2. DG-012-A: bridge-count odd-run bound (PROVED; any fixed mountain tail)

**Proposition.** If a fixed ordinary positive seed \(n=a_0\) follows \(j+1\) consecutive strict mountain record bridges, with sources \(a_j\), then
\[
2^{s_j}\le a_j+1 \le (3/2)^j(n+1),\qquad
r_j<\mu^2 j+\mu\log_2(n+1),\quad \mu=\log_2(3/2).
\tag{2}
\]
Numerically \(\mu^2=0.34218112724994865\). Consequently any infinite mountain itinerary on one positive n satisfies \(\limsup r_j/j\le\mu^2\).

**Proof.** The odd-run divisibility yields \(2^{s_j}\mid a_j+1\). Strict record geometry gives
\(a_{j+1}<T(a_j)=(3a_j+1)/2\), hence \(a_{j+1}+1<(3/2)(a_j+1)\), and iteration yields the middle bound. By definition \(s_j>(\log2/\log(3/2))r_j=r_j/\mu\). Take base-2 logarithms and rearrange. No elapsed-time exponent \(t_j\) is used. \(\square\)

This excludes e.g. \(r_j=5+6\lfloor j/12\rfloor\) (asymptotic slope \(1/2\)), whether or not its separate total-time DG011 restriction fires. It does not by itself exclude \(r_j=5+6\lfloor j/30\rfloor\) (slope \(1/5\)).

## 3. DG-012-B: exact repeated-word divisibility and global nonrealisation (PROVED)

**Repeat lemma.** Suppose \(K\ge1\) consecutive strict bridges all use the same \(w_r\), beginning at positive integer \(x\). Then
\[
D^K\mid (A-D)x+q,\qquad D^{K-1}<x+1.
\tag{3}
\]
**Proof.** Set \(C=A-D>0\). The exact affine identity
\[
C F_r(z)+q=(A/D)(Cz+q)
\]
iterates to \(C F_r^K(x)+q=(A/D)^K(Cx+q)\). Both end terms are integers, and \(\gcd(A,D)=1\), hence \(D^K\mid Cx+q\). Also \(0<C<D/2,\ 0<q<3D/2\), so for \(x\ge1\)
\[
0<Cx+q<D(x/2+3/2)\le D(x+1).
\]
A positive multiple of \(D^K\) below \(D(x+1)\) requires \(D^{K-1}<x+1\). \(\square\)

**Theorem (eventually nondecreasing mountain obstruction).** There is **no** ordinary positive integer whose actual shortened-Collatz orbit has an infinite suffix made of consecutive strict \(w_{r_j}\)-bridges with \(r_j\equiv5\bmod6,\ r_j\ge5\), where \(r_j\) is eventually nondecreasing.

**Proof.** Discard any finite initial bridges, replace the starting positive integer by the current record, and assume nondecreasing from index zero. If \(r_j\) eventually stabilises at \(r\), one initial positive \(x\) would follow \(K\) copies of \(w_r\) for every K, contradicting (3) as \(D>1\).

Otherwise write \(\rho_k\) for the k-th **distinct** plateau value, \(J_k\) for the starting bridge index of that plateau, and \(K_k=J_{k+1}-J_k\ge1\) for its finite length. Nondecreasing \(5\bmod6\) values give \(\rho_k\ge5+6k\). Put \(H=\log_2(n+1)\). At plateau start, \(2^{s(\rho_k)}\mid a_{J_k}+1\) and the strict-bridge estimate gives
\[
2^{\rho_k}<2^{s(\rho_k)}\le a_{J_k}+1\le 2^{J_k}(n+1).
\]
Therefore
\[
J_k>\rho_k-H\ge5+6k-H,\qquad
\liminf_{k\to\infty}J_k/k\ge6. \tag{4}
\]
On the other hand applying the repeat lemma (3) to that plateau and using \(D=2^{s+\rho_k}\) gives
\[
(K_k-1)(s+\rho_k)<\log_2(a_{J_k}+1)\le J_k+H,
\]
and hence
\[
J_{k+1}\le J_k+1+\frac{J_k+H}{5+6k}. \tag{5}
\]
For clarity, (5) first implies \(J_k=O(k)\): with \(B=H+5\), induction from \(J_0=0\) shows \(J_k\le B(k+1)\), since \((k+1)/(5+6k)\le1/5\) and \(1+(B+H)/5\le B\). Let \(L=\limsup_{k\to\infty}J_k/k<\infty\). From (5),
\(\limsup K_k\le1+L/6\); averaging the nonnegative integers \(K_i\) gives \(L\le1+L/6\), so \(L\le6/5\). This contradicts (4). \(\square\)

**Why this is stronger than the single-run test.** The explicit slowly rising staircase
\[
r_j=5+6\lfloor j/30\rfloor
\]
has \(r_j/j\to1/5<\mu^2\), so (2) by itself produces no asymptotic contradiction. But at plateau index \(J_k=30k\) the word repeats \(K=30\) times and (3) forces
\[
2^{29(s(5+6k)+5+6k)}< a_{30k}+1\le2^{30k}(n+1).
\]
Because \(s(r)>r\), the exponent on the left minus \(30k\) tends to infinity, an explicit fixed-n contradiction. Every **finite** initial segment nonetheless has infinitely many strict positive bridge realisations (DG011). This is a genuine cross-bridge *real growth/divisibility* obstruction, not the bare algebraic fact of cylinder compatibility. We do NOT infer that any fixed seed follows the infinite compatible 2-adic path.
