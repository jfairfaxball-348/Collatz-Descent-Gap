## 1. A square-free bounded-alphabet adversary (DG-014-A, PROVED using the classical Thue word)

Recall the mountain family w_r = 1^{s(r)}0^r, where r >= 5, r = 5 (mod 6), s(r) is the least positive s with (3/2)^s > 2^r, and the bridge map has slope lambda_r = 3^{s(r)}/2^{s(r)+r} strictly between 1 and 3/2. Every finite list of these words admits infinitely many *ordinary positive integer* starts n = 0 (mod 3) realising consecutive strict increasing local bridges, with each endpoint 7 (mod 9) (DG010–DG011). This is not a single seed following an infinite schedule.

Let u = u_0 u_1 ... be the classical infinite **square-free ternary Thue word**, the fixed point starting at 0 of the substitution 0 -> 012, 1 -> 02, 2 -> 1. Its square-freeness is a known classical theorem, **imported** here, not reproved or claimed original; e.g. Badkobeh–Ochem, *More characterizations of morphic words*, Theoretical Computer Science (2026), https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304397526001349. Map 0,1,2 injectively to the mountain parameters 5,11,17. This yields an infinite schedule over the **three-letter bounded alphabet {5,11,17}** with no factor VV, where V is any nonempty finite block of mountain parameters.

**Why the proposed general repetition gate fails.** A DG013 periodic island requires K >= 2 consecutive repetitions of one fixed *compound mountain superword*. Such a factor would imply a square VV in the parameter word: concatenations of mountains are unambiguously parsed by their alternating nonempty maximal 1-runs and 0-runs. The Thue schedule has no such factor anywhere. Therefore no all-infinite-mountain-word theorem can rely merely on existence of sufficiently large consecutive repeated superwords. In fact **none** exist in this example. This refutes the *combinatorial forcing proposal*, not the unproved assertion that no ordinary positive integer can follow this schedule forever.

## 2. Sparse spikes: an unbounded genuinely oscillating square-free adversary (DG-014-B, PROVED)

To challenge both a finite-alphabet and unbounded irregular itinerary, retain the above ternary schedule except at indices j_k = 2^{k+8}, k >= 0, and replace its value there by r_{j_k} = 23+6k. These new symbols lie outside {5,11,17}, are pairwise distinct and remain 5 (mod 6). The resulting infinite parameter word is **square-free**: a supposed square either uses only original unmodified alphabet positions (giving a square in the original Thue word) or contains at least one unique spike symbol, which cannot occur in both equal halves. It is unbounded and has arbitrarily large one-step drops immediately after spikes. Also

    r_j/j -> 0 as j -> infinity,

since outside spikes r_j <= 17, while at j=2^{k+8} the ratio is (23+6k)/2^{k+8} -> 0. Thus it satisfies DG012's necessary eventual slope bound limsup r_j/j <= (log_2(3/2))^2 with substantial room. Every finite prefix has infinitely many genuine positive strict-bridge realisations n = 0 (mod 3) by the finite cylinder/CRT argument in DG011. **This does not exhibit any one positive integer realising the infinite word;** the compatible infinite parity stream is a 2-adic object unless a fixed ordinary positive seed is established.

In particular the potentially strongest proposed special gate "every infinite strict mountain itinerary contains an adequately long repeated compound block" is **FALSE as a statement about symbolic mountain itineraries**, even with unbounded oscillations, finite positive shadows and sublinear odd-run demand. It could still be true **conditional on the entire infinite word actually being followed by one positive integer**; that strengthened assertion is not decided by this construction and must not be declared false.

Exact tests: the checker constructs and explicitly simulates valid ordinary integer witnesses of the first N = 36, 258, 530 mountain blocks, with parity lengths respectively 1092, 7842, 16108. The first two spikes are at indices 256 (r=23) and 512 (r=29); the sample seed decimal-digit counts are 330, 2362, 4850. The checker directly checks square-freeness of those parameter prefixes and the strict inequality for every interior iterate of every bridge. Finite checks alone do not prove infinite square-freeness or an infinite orbit; the infinite symbolic theorem uses the cited Thue theorem and the unique-symbol argument.

## 3. No uniform improvement to the record growth ceiling from finite strict geometry (DG-014-C, PROVED)

DG012–DG013 use the valid actual strict-record inequality b+1 < (3/2)(a+1), hence b < T(a) for an odd bridge source a. We show the coefficient 3/2 cannot be replaced by any fixed smaller number based **solely on the properties of a finite strict increasing bridge**.

Put mu = log_2(3/2). Then mu is irrational: if mu=p/q rational, 3^q=2^{p+q}, impossible by prime factorisation. By minimality s(r)=floor(r/mu)+1 (there is no integer equality). Hence

    log_2(lambda_r) = mu*(1 - {r/mu}),   0 < log_2(lambda_r) < mu.

For r=5+6h, h >= 0, the fractional parts {r/mu} are dense in [0,1] by the elementary irrational-rotation theorem (6/mu irrational). Consequently lambda_r comes arbitrarily close to 3/2 from below. For a chosen r, the affine mountain endpoint is b=lambda_r*a+eta_r with fixed eta_r>0. Along the infinite arithmetic progression of genuine large positive strict witnesses n = 0 (mod 3), b/a -> lambda_r. Thus for every epsilon>0 there exist arbitrarily large finite strict mountain bridges, with endpoints 7 (mod 9), for which b/a > 3/2-epsilon. No universal local theorem b <= (3/2-epsilon)a for *all finite strict bridges* can hold.

The exact checker exhibits r=4847, s=8287, lambda_r approximately 1.499252601556, less than 0.001 below 3/2; the universal sharpness result follows from irrational rotation, not this single numerical case. **Quantifier warning:** finite strict mountain bridges need not lie on actual E orbits. A stronger bound holding *only for actual exceptional record bridges* has NOT been disproved. No density-incompatible E count follows.

## 4. Bounded/preperiodic E: exact cycle first-entrance and a 3-divisible precursor (DG-014-D, PROVED CONDITIONALLY)

For an ordinary positive cycle C of the shortened map, any *first entry* T(x)=y in C with x outside C must satisfy y=2 (mod 3). Indeed a positive y not 2 (mod 3) has the unique positive immediate predecessor 2y, which is already its cycle predecessor, so cannot have a different external predecessor. When y=2 (mod 3), the two possible immediate predecessors are 2y (even) and (2y-1)/3 (odd). This is exact predecessor rigidity, not an existence theorem for a nontrivial cycle.

Let c>1 be the minimum of a **hypothetical** positive nontrivial cycle. It must be odd (an even c would halve to a smaller member). If c=2 (mod 3), the odd integer z=(2c-1)/3 satisfies 0<z<c and T(z)=c. It is therefore **outside** C, but z belongs to E: after its first step every iterate belongs to C and is at least c>z. More specifically, if c=5 (mod 9), z is divisible by 3. Thus a *hypothetical* nontrivial cycle with minimum 5 (mod 9) would automatically produce a smaller **3-divisible preperiodic exceptional integer**. The modular examples (c,z)=(5,3),(23,15),(41,27),(59,39) verify the one-step identity only; none of these c is asserted to be a cycle minimum.

This is a sharp logical reason why "cycles have no multiples of 3" cannot eliminate 3-divisible *preperiodic* E elements. Nor does it establish that a least E member itself is 3-divisible: it could be a different earlier exceptional integer. The existence of nontrivial positive cycles, or their minima in 5 (mod 9), is unproved; bounded E remains completely unresolved.

## 5. Unrestricted genuine E-bridge and inverse-root audit

An arbitrary unbounded genuine E orbit need not use mountain parity words at all. DG011's seven-odd-step shield for b=7 (mod 9) and DG010's two-gap first-meeting window are respected by all the finite examples. Since the tested target endpoints are 7 (mod 9), none triggers the known shallow target families 2 (mod 3), 4 (mod 9), or 10 (mod 81). The sharpness theorem above shows that a uniform factor improvement cannot be deduced from *finite bridge strictness alone*, but it does not rule out an actual-E restriction. No recurrence of genuinely divergent short inverse roots or collision-free positive-density bound has been proved. Counts of sparse roots or finite witnesses remain consistent with Terras–Everett's natural-density-zero E theorem. A 2-adic parity cylinder is not a positive exceptional integer.
