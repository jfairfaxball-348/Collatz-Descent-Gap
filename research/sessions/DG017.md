# DG017 — E-specific weighted parity collision and inverse-root audit

**EXECUTED 16 October 2026.** Sole target: prove E empty, where E is the set of positive integers m >= 2 such that T^t(m) >= m for every t >= 1, with T even = n/2 and T odd = (3n+1)/2. **E empty is NOT PROVED.** The results below are elementary paper arguments plus finite exact checks, not Lean or Palomar.

## 1. DG-017-A — actual E-orbit weighted collision theorem (PROVED, scoped)

For a fixed m in E, write x_t = T^t(m), let s_t be the number of odd states x_i for i<t, and e_t=t-s_t. Define

\[
B_m=\frac{m+2}{2(m+1)}\in(1/2,1),\qquad
W_m(t)=\left(\frac32\right)^{s_t} B_m^{e_t}.
\tag{1}
\]

**Exact product and upper bound.** Every finite prefix of this true positive orbit satisfies

\[
\frac{x_t+1}{m+1}
 =\left(\frac32\right)^{s_t}
 \prod_{\substack{0\le i<t\\x_i\ {\rm even}}}
 \frac{x_i+2}{2(x_i+1)}
 \le W_m(t).
\tag{2}
\]

Proof: on odd states, (T(x)+1)/(x+1)=3/2 exactly. On even states the ratio is (x+2)/(2(x+1)), which decreases as x increases. Because m lies in E, all x_i>=m, giving an even-step factor at most B_m. Multiply the exact ratios. The integer-only form, suitable for independent Lean work, is

\[
(x_t+1)\,2^t (m+1)^{e_t}
 \le (m+1)3^{s_t}(m+2)^{e_t}.
\tag{3}
\]

**Return/collision theorem.** Further suppose that the orbit of m is unbounded, so no state can repeat (else it would be eventually periodic and bounded). Let equal length-h parity factors begin at 0<=p<q. The parity-cylinder bijection implies 2^h divides x_q-x_p. The states are distinct, so |x_q-x_p|>=2^h. As both are >=m, their larger value is >=m+2^h. Bound that larger value by (2) at its **own time index**. It follows that

\[
\boxed{
\max\{W_m(p), W_m(q)\}
 \ge 1+\frac{2^h}{m+1}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

The max is essential. This is a genuine infinite positive-orbit necessary restriction **conditional on E containing an unbounded element**. It makes no mountain decomposition or bounded-alphabet assumption. Because W_m(t)<=(3/2)^t, the bound is strictly tighter at numerous even-rich collisions than DG016's crude (3/2)^q upper envelope.

**Complexity consequence.** Let P_m(h) count the distinct binary parity factors of length h along this infinite orbit and put

\[
A_m(h)=\{t\ge0: W_m(t)<1+2^h/(m+1)\}.
\]

Two times from A_m(h) cannot begin the same factor by (4), hence

\[
|A_m(h)|\le P_m(h)\le 2^h.
\tag{5}
\]

In particular W_m(t) tends to infinity as t grows: every fixed sublevel set is finite. This is **not** many exceptional integers. It counts positions in one word. In fact an injective unbounded positive-integer orbit has x_t tending to infinity already, so this drift statement is not an independent breakthrough.

**Known comparison:** E itself gives the prefix odd-step density floor

\[
2^t\le(3+1/m)^{s_t},
\tag{6}
\]

by the exact even multiplier 1/2 and odd multiplier at most (3+1/m)/2. This is substantially covered by the independent formally verified density_floor in Shadow.lean; (6) is **not claimed original**. Nor does (4) prove a better unconditional asymptotic slope for P_m(h) than DG016's 1/log_2(3/2).

## 2. DG-017-K — incorrect later-time-only inequality KILLED

It is **false**, even for a genuine positive finite non-descending trace, to replace (4) by W_m(q)>=1+2^h/(m+1) unconditionally. Take

\[
3\longrightarrow5\longrightarrow8\longrightarrow4.
\]

The first four states are all >=m=3. Take p=2, q=3 and h=1: both parity factors are 0, but the larger state is at the **earlier** index. Here B_3=5/8, W_3(2)=9/4, and W_3(3)=45/32. Yet 1+2/4=3/2 >45/32. The max in (4) works. The later-time version becomes valid only when separately proving x_q>=x_p. Do not identify elapsed parity-factor time q with a record index.

For each finite length L, the seed 2^L-1 follows L odd steps, so e_t=0 and the even-step penalty vanishes throughout those first L steps. These are actual positive finite shadows with no universal infinite implication. A 2-adic infinite all-odd word is not a positive ordinary-integer exceptional orbit.

## 3. DG-017-B — high complexity does not generate a disjoint inverse forest

Recode the ternary Champernowne word through the strict mountain parameters 0->5, 1->11, 2->17. Its parameter-factor complexity is exactly 3^h for each h (every length-h ternary word occurs). DG010/11's CRT and exact parity-cylinder constructions give infinitely many **positive finite** strict mountain realizations for each prescribed finite prefix, with initial seed divisible by 3 and every finished mountain endpoint 7 mod 9. The independent DG017 checker simulates initial prefixes of 5, 12 and 30 blocks exactly, with source bit lengths 126, 305 and 819.

These endpoints avoid the previously counted classes 2 mod 3, 4 mod 9, and 10 mod 81. They do **not** eliminate deep alternative inverses. DG011 shows that an expanding inverse into a 7 mod 9 endpoint requires at least seven odd steps and at least eleven total steps. DG010's collision-free off-spine inverse-root argument separately requires a truly integral strict-safe divergent inverse, with length ell_j<d_(j-1)+d_j, and a proof of its first meeting inside the relevant two-gap window. Having exponentially many **factors of one word** does not create such roots or many **different integers in E**. Even a lower bound of order log X on distinct roots would remain consistent with Terras–Everett's E=o(X).

**Negative conclusion:** High symbolic complexity plus all these finite positive shadows defeats any claimed *finite-horizon* forcing of one of the existing three shallow inverse-root classes. It is not an example of one positive integer following the entire infinite Champernowne mountain stream. Whether such an infinite realization exists is not decided here.

## 4. DG-017-C — bounded/preperiodic least exception and full-cycle integrality

Let a hypothetical nontrivial positive periodic cycle have a length-L parity word with s odd symbols and standard affine numerator Q>0:
\[
2^L T^L(y)=3^s y+Q.
\]
A true periodic state y>0 must satisfy
\[
(2^L-3^s)y=Q,\quad 2^L>3^s,\quad (2^L-3^s)\mid Q.
\tag{7}
\]
These are the classical necessary positive-cycle divisibility conditions; no nontrivial positive cycle is asserted to exist.

A first entry from **outside** the cycle must be at y=2 mod 3: otherwise the only positive predecessor 2y already belongs to the cycle. If m=min E were preperiodic to this cycle and entered after t steps with finite prefix word v and its affine data (3^{s_v},q_v,2^t), then
\[
m=(2^t y-q_v)/3^{s_v}.
\]
In addition, every pre-entry orbit value is >=m and m<=c, the minimum cycle value (the future orbit visits c). DG015's genuine least-E 2-mod-3 visit floor further says y>=T(m), not a condition available to arbitrary finite survivors. These relations coexist; no congruence impossibility across all possible cycles follows. The genuine finite trace 15->23->35->53->80->40->20->10 has an intermediate 2-mod-3 value 20 below T(15)=23, disproving a *finite-shadow* version of the floor but not the actual least-E theorem. A possible 3-divisible least exceptional seed feeding into an unknown nontrivial cycle is still OPEN.
