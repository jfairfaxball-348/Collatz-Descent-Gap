\[
a \equiv A_d^{-1}(3\cdot 2^d-q_d)\pmod{2^{d+2}},

## DG-007-B: safe off-spine root from a 1-mod-3 target (PROVED, CONDITIONAL)

**Safe 110-predecessor lemma (general E statement).** Let \(b\ge13\) be odd and \(b\equiv4\pmod9\). Define

\[
z=\frac{8b-5}{9},\qquad y=\frac{4b-1}{3}.
\]

These are odd positive integers with \(3\le z<b<y<2b\), and the **actual** orbit prefix has
\[
z\xrightarrow{1}y\xrightarrow{1}2b\xrightarrow{0}b.
\]
Hence \(b\in E\Rightarrow z\in E\): for times 1, 2, 3 the iterates are all \(>z\); after time 3, iterates from \(b\in E\) are \(\ge b>z\). This recovers the three-step strict-prefix safe-inverse mechanism for all E members of this residue class, including bounded E-orbits.

**New record/spine application.** If \(b=a_{j+1}\) is an actual exceptional suffix minimum after a gap \(d_j\ge6\) and \(b\equiv4\pmod9\), then this \(z\in E\) is **off the original unbounded orbit**. Were it on the orbit, orbit injectivity forces its occurrence precisely three steps before \(b\), strictly inside the preceding gap. But that value \(z<b\) contradicts the fact that every intermediate value is \(>b\). (At gap 3 the predecessor is exactly \(z=a_j\), so it is **not** an extra point.)

The first orbit intersection of the off-spine \(z\) is one of \(y,2b,b\), occurring in the final two time steps or at the record itself, hence in the half-open time interval \((t_j,t_{j+1}]\). Inverse roots associated with *different* record gaps have first spine-intersection times in disjoint such intervals. The old DG006 odd roots from \(2\pmod3\) targets have first intersection exactly at their targets. Thus the newly certified roots are distinct from the records, distinct from one another, and distinct from all DG006 weighted roots.

Writing \(R(X)=\#\{j:a_j\le X\}\), and
\[
Q_4(X)=\#\{j:a_{j+1}\le X,\ d_j\ge6,\ a_{j+1}\equiv4\pmod9\},
\]
the additive conditional theorem is
\[
\boxed{|E\cap[2,X]|\ge R(X)+
\sum_{\substack{j:a_{j+1}\le X\\d_j\ge3,\ a_{j+1}\equiv2\ (3)}}
v_3(a_{j+1}+1)+Q_4(X),\qquad X\ge n.}
\]
Unlike DG006's weighted sum, \(Q_4\) counts certain targets **congruent to 1 mod 3**. The length-9 cylinder 111110010 gives finite illustrative values
\(a=223+2048h\), \(b=319+2916h\), \(z=283+2592h<b\); the finite examples do **not** demonstrate any actual exceptional orbit. Neither \(Q_4>0\) nor any nonzero asymptotic frequency is proved, so **no density contradiction follows**.

**Least-exception first-transition gate.** Suppose \(m=\min E\) has an unbounded orbit and its **first record transition** has \(d\ge6\). If \(m\equiv3\pmod8\), its first three parity bits are 110, giving \(T^3(m)=(9m+5)/8>b\). A target \(b\equiv4\pmod9\) would yield the safe root \(z=(8b-5)/9<m\), contradiction. Therefore such a first transition cannot simultaneously have \(m\equiv3\pmod8\) and \(b\equiv4\pmod9\).

For instance the locally valid length-11 bridge word **11011110010** has
\(m=7579+8192h\to b=8095+8748h\equiv4\pmod9\); its safe 110 root \(7195+7776h<m\). This whole *first-transition cylinder* is forbidden for a least exceptional integer with unbounded orbit, even though these are bona fide positive finite bridges. This is not an E-free progression: the premise includes an unproved exceptional-record endpoint.

**Proof.** In each displayed word every prefix satisfies \(3^{s_i}\ge2^i\), and the final multiplier is strict \(2187>2048\). Let \(m\ge3\) be any **odd** integer in its listed congruence class. Then
\[
z=(2048m-q_{11})/2187
