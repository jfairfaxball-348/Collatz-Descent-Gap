## DG-009-C: a non-eventually-periodic infinite mixed 2-adic schedule that NO positive integer follows (PROVED NEGATIVE THEOREM)

For a single u block F(x)=(729x+881)/512, a positive integer x following u **t consecutive times** necessarily satisfies

512^t | (217x+881),

because expanding the affine iterate and cancelling the odd factor 729^t modulo 512^t gives exactly this congruence. Thus 512^t<=217x+881. At the start of block J on an ordinary positive orbit starting at n, the elementary bound T^k(n)+1 <= (3/2)^k(n+1) implies

512^t <= 217 (3/2)^(9J)(n+1) + 881.       (*)

Construct an infinite alphabet word W=v u^(J1+1) v u^(J2+1) v ... with J1=1 and J_(i+1)=2 J_i+2; J_i denotes the number of completed nine-step blocks before the displayed long u run. The run at J_i has t=J_i+1. If one fixed positive n followed this word, (*) would hold for unbounded J_i; but 512^2>3^9, so 512^(J_i+1)/[(3/2)^(9J_i)] grows exponentially to infinity. Contradiction. The block sequence is genuinely varying and not eventually periodic.

Nonetheless every finite prefix block list admits infinitely many positive integer strict bridge realisations, including 0(mod3) starts, by the CRT and positive-slope argument in DG-009-A. Their nested parity cylinders define a 2-adic integer, but the infinite word has **no ordinary positive-integer realisation**. This is a precise falsification of an infinite-cylinder or arbitrary-finite-schedule-to-positive-exception argument, not a construction of E or a theorem barring every other infinite mixed u/v schedule.

The separate classical eventually-periodic-parity obstruction also rules out any eventually-periodic u/v schedule on an actual positive orbit; it does not rule out all nonperiodic schedules. One cannot claim all infinite u/v schedules impossible from the specific long-run example.
