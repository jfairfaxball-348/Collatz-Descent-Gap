## 1. Actual orbit record transitions: DG-005-A (PROVED)

Write E={n>=2: T^j(n)>=n for every j>=1}. Assume an **unbounded** positive-integer orbit x_i=T^i(n), with n in E. Let a_0=n, and a_j=x_{t_j} be its successive strict suffix minima as in DG004. Thus t_0=0<t_1<..., all a_j in E, a_j<a_{j+1}, and a_j is the minimum of the tail beginning at t_j. Define d_j=t_{j+1}-t_j.

**Lemma 1 (3-adic gate).** If x_0 is odd, then 3 does not divide x_i for any i>=1. Indeed the first odd step produces T(x_0)=(3x_0+1)/2 congruent to 2 modulo 3. From any non-multiple of 3, an even step divides by the unit 2 modulo 3, and an odd step again outputs 2 modulo 3. Induction proves the claim. In particular, all a_j for j>=1 are congruent to 1 or 2 modulo 3, even if a_0 is divisible by 3. **This is not an exclusion of 3|a_0.**

**Lemma 2 (transition geometry).** Every d_j belongs to {1,3,4,...}; **d_j=2 is impossible**. When d_j=1, we have a_{j+1}=T(a_j) congruent to 2 modulo 3 and a_j congruent to 7 modulo 8. When d_j>=3, the last step arriving at b=a_{j+1} is an even step from 2b.

Proof: For d>=2 the penultimate value x_{t_{j+1}-1} occurs after t_j, hence is at least b by the definition of the next suffix minimum. Every odd one-step predecessor of b is (2b-1)/3<b for b>=2; therefore the penultimate value must be the even predecessor 2b. If d=2, T(a_j)=2b; either a_j=4b (even) or a_j=(4b-1)/3 (odd), both strictly bigger than b, contradicting a_j<b. If d=1, the initial a_j is odd because it belongs to E; hence the next term is 2 mod 3. Both a_j and b lie in E and therefore are 3 mod 4. Writing a_j=4u+3 and b=6u+5 shows u odd, i.e. a_j=7 mod 8. QED.

**Lemma 3 (infinite-horizon long gaps).** Infinitely many d_j are >=3. Otherwise, since d_j cannot be 2, all sufficiently late gaps equal 1. The resulting orbit tail consists only of E-elements, hence only odd integers. But t consecutive odd steps from a positive start u require 2^t | (u+1), impossible for arbitrarily large t. More quantitatively, from a record a with v_2(a+1)=v, a run of h immediately consecutive record transitions (d=1) must have h<=v-2, since every terminal record remains 3 mod 4. This proves infinitely many long gaps, **not a positive frequency of them**.

These facts use the exact ordering and predecessor structure of a genuine unbounded orbit. They do not assert existence of such an orbit.

## 2. Safe inverse roots outside the orbit: DG-005-B (PROVED, CONDITIONAL)

Take any later tail minimum b=a_{j+1} congruent to 2 mod 3. It is necessarily odd and >=5. Define z_b=(2b-1)/3. Then z_b is an odd integer with 2<=z_b<b and T(z_b)=b. Since b is in E, the safe-ancestor transfer theorem (DG003) proves z_b in E. This applies to **all** such b, independently of d_j.

If moreover d_j>=3, Lemma 2 says the *actual* predecessor of b on the given orbit is 2b, not z_b. The unbounded orbit has no repeated values, so z_b cannot occur anywhere on that orbit: otherwise its next value would give a second occurrence of b. The map b -> (2b-1)/3 is injective; consequently different long-gap, 2-mod-3 records give **different exceptional integers off the original orbit**.

For X>=n put R(X)=#{j>=0:a_j<=X}, and Q(X)=#{j>=0:a_{j+1}<=X, d_j>=3, a_{j+1}=2 mod 3}. Then

\[
|E\cap[2,X]|\ \ge\ R(X)+Q(X).
\]

This improves the earlier record-only count whenever Q(X)>0. It is rigorously a **conditional counting theorem**: the unknown Q(X) may be zero, and even Q(X) comparable to R(X)=O(log X) would not contradict density zero. If d_j=1, z_b=a_j and cannot be counted again. If b=1 mod 3, there is no analogous one-step odd inverse root. If b=0 mod 3, Lemma 1 rules it out for j>=1.

**Fixed-word limitation.** For any fixed inverse parity word w, write at prefix length i the affine expression T^i(z)=(3^{s_i}z+q_i)/2^i, q_i>=0. If a prefix has 3^{s_i}<2^i, a no-drop bridge must obey

\[
z\le q_i/(2^i-3^{s_i}).
\]

Thus that fixed word has only finitely many possible safe positive starting roots at all. This does **not** forbid such finite small-root bridges: it shows they cannot generate an unbounded arithmetic progression of safe roots along the *same* word. DG004's strict-prefix-expansion condition remains sufficient for an unbounded progression. In particular, enumerating arbitrary finite bridges does not by itself establish growth in Q(X).

**Scope:** these b_j are a mathematical sequence of candidate record *values*, not verified Collatz transitions. They invalidate only attempts to force positive density from those inequalities and residue exclusions alone. ## 4. Variable-depth whole-E-disjoint progressions: DG-005-D (PROVED)

This meets the session's infinite-family challenge without promoting finite-prefix algebra to a universal proof. For every k>=2 set

\[
r_k=3^{k-1},\quad
t_k=(2^{r_k}+1)/3^k,\quad
N_k=2^k t_k-1,\quad
M_k=2^{k+r_k}.
\]

The integer t_k exists because the elementary lifting-the-exponent identity gives v_3(2^{3^{k-1}}+1)=k (or prove inductively by factoring A^3+1). We have N_k>=3, N_k odd, and N_k<2^{r_k}.

**Theorem (parameter-unbounded descent progressions).** Every n=N_k+M_k h, h>=0, follows k odd steps, then r_k even steps. Exactly,

\[
T^j(n)=3^j 2^{k-j}(t_k+2^{r_k}h)-1\quad(0\le j\le k),\qquad
T^{k+r_k}(n)=1+3^k h<n.
\]

Thus the **entire infinite arithmetic progression** N_k mod M_k is disjoint from E, with k<sigma(n)<=k+r_k for all h>=0. The lower bound is uniform in the progression and tends to infinity with k. For the seed N_k itself, since all values after step k are descending powers of 2,

\[
\sigma(N_k)=k+r_k-\lfloor\log_2 N_k\rfloor.
\]

Proof: the j<=k affine identity follows by induction from n=2^k(t_k+2^{r_k}h)-1: each pre-step is odd. At j=k the value is 2^{r_k}(1+3^k h), so the next r_k steps are even and end at 1+3^k h. Since M_k>3^k and N_k>1, the end value is below n for every h>=0. The first k iterates strictly grow from n, giving sigma>k. For h=0 the powers-of-two tail yields the exact first time below odd N_k. QED.

Small exact examples: k=2 gives r=3, N=3, sigma=4; k=3 gives r=9, N=151, sigma=5; k=4 gives r=27, N=26512143, sigma=7.

**Crucial limitation:** for each fixed k this is a finite parity-cylinder descent certificate. Varying k yields unbounded first-descent times *on different starting integers*, not a common positive integer surviving every depth. It does not exclude a previously unresolved E-member or advance the global density-zero-to-empty inference. It is a rigorous infinite-family result, not the bridge.
