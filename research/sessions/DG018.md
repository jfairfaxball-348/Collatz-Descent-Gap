# DG018 — Safe side-entrance roots and record-index parity rigidity

**EXECUTED 16 October 2026.** Shortened map \(T(2u)=u,\ T(2u+1)=3u+2\). Sole objective \(E=\{m\ge2:T^t(m)\ge m\ \forall t\ge1\}=\varnothing\). **E empty is NOT proved.** Results below are elementary paper proofs conditional on explicitly stated hypotheses, plus exact finite tests; not independently novel, Lean-certified, or publication-ready.

## DG-018-A — an integral shorter strict-safe sibling of every rising odd-run/even-tail bridge (PROVED)

**Theorem.** Assume \(s\ge4,\ r\ge2\), and an **ordinary positive integer** \(a\) actually follows the parity word \(1^s0^r\) with endpoint \(b=T^{s+r}(a)>a\). Then

\[
z=\frac{3a-1}{4}\in\mathbb Z,\qquad 2\le z<a,
\qquad T^{s+r-2}(z)=b,
\]

and the exact parity word of \(z\) to \(b\) is \(1^{s-2}010^{r-2}\). Every intermediate value on this shorter path is **strictly greater than \(z\)**. Its first *aligned* coalescence with the original word is

\[
T^s(z)=T^{s+2}(a)=2^{r-2}b,
\quad T^p(z)\ne T^{p+2}(a)\quad(0\le p<s).
\]

In particular this shorter inverse is a genuinely different branch, not an initial prefix followed by the original bridge.

**Proof.** The first \(s\) odds give \(a=2^s k-1\) with positive integer \(k\), and the \(r\) final evens give \(3^s k-1=2^r b\). Hence \(3^s k\equiv1\pmod4\). Then \(z=3\cdot2^{s-2}k-1\) is integral and \(2\le z<a\). Its first \(s-2\) steps are odd, with

\[
T^p(z)=3^{p+1}2^{s-2-p}k-1\qquad(0\le p\le s-2).
\]

This ends at the even integer \(3^{s-1}k-1\). One halving gives \(u=(3^{s-1}k-1)/2\), which is odd because \(3^{s-1}k\equiv3\pmod4\). Its next odd step reaches \((3^s k-1)/4=2^{r-2}b\), then \(r-2\) halvings reach \(b\). All initial odd steps increase; moreover
\[
u-z=\frac{(3^{s-1}-3\cdot2^{s-1})k+1}{2}>0\qquad(s\ge4),
\]
and all remaining values are at least \(b>a>z\). Finally for \(p\le s-2\), the aligned original state equals \(3^{p+2}2^{s-2-p}k-1\), not \(T^p(z)\); for \(p=s-1\), the aligned states are \((3^s k-1)/2\) and \((3^{s-1}k-1)/2\), distinct. They first agree at \(p=s\). \(\square\)

**Small finite check:** \(15\to23\to35\to53\to80\to40\to20\) follows \(1^40^2\). Its smaller sibling \(11\to17\to26\to13\to20\) follows \(1^201\). Both are finite traces; neither is claimed to be in E. All DG010–DG011 mountain words \(w_r=1^{s(r)}0^r\), \(r\ge5,\ r\equiv5\pmod6\), satisfy the theorem's word hypotheses.

## DG-018-B — disjoint first meetings and an extra E-root for every actual qualifying record bridge (PROVED CONDITIONALLY)

Suppose \(m\in E\) has an **unbounded** ordinary positive-integer orbit \(x_t=T^t(m)\). This deterministic orbit is injective. Let \(a_j=x_{t_j}\) be its successive *actual suffix minima*, starting \(a_0=m,t_0=0\), with increasing values and record gaps \(d_j=t_{j+1}-t_j\). Every record value \(a_j\) is itself in E.

If an actual record bridge \(a_j\to a_{j+1}=b\) has word \(1^{s_j}0^{r_j}\) with \(s_j\ge4,r_j\ge2\), theorem A gives
\[
z_j=(3a_j-1)/4,\quad 2\le z_j<a_j<b,
\quad T^{d_j-2}(z_j)=b,
\]
with strict safety. Since \(b\in E\), **\(z_j\in E\)**.

Any equality \(T^p(z_j)=x_t\) for \(0\le p\le d_j-2\) forces, by forward iteration to \(b=x_{t_{j+1}}\) and injectivity of the original orbit,
\[
t=t_{j+1}-(d_j-2)+p=t_j+2+p.
\]
Theorem A rules this out for \(p<s_j\) and verifies it at \(p=s_j\). Thus \(z_j\) is **not** on the original orbit, and its first original-orbit meeting is at the exact index \(t_j+s_j+2\), inside \((t_j,t_{j+1}]\). Such first-meeting indices are disjoint for distinct bridges. Hence all side roots \(z_j\) are distinct and off-spine: no two-gap inequality or alternating-index thinning is necessary **for this constructed family**.

Define \(R_m(X)=\#\{j:a_j\le X\}\), and \(M_m(X)\) as the number of qualifying record bridges with \(a_{j+1}\le X\). Since \(2\le z_j<a_j<a_{j+1}\le X\),

\[
\boxed{\#(E\cap[2,X])\ge R_m(X)+M_m(X).}
\]

**Effect and limitation.** A genuinely high-complexity mountain orbit would supply one such side root *per mountain bridge*, including 7-mod-9 targets. This refutes interpreting the DG011 shallow expanding-inverse shield as the absence of **all** divergent safe inverses: the constructed sibling has \(s(r)-1\ge8\) odds and length \(s(r)+r-2\ge12\), safely above its shield. But **no theorem forces even one mountain record bridge on arbitrary E orbits**, and infinite finite-prefix-realisation of Champernowne mountains is not one fixed positive seed. Even \(M_m(X)=R_m(X)-1\) gives only a possible logarithmic-order improvement, compatible with the external Terras–Everett \(\#(E\cap[2,X])=o(X)\).

**Least E:** If \(m=\min E\) and its orbit is unbounded, its very first actual suffix-record bridge cannot be \(1^s0^r\) in the above range: then \(z_0<m\) would itself lie in E. This excludes that specific initial bridge shape, **not** all initial words or all exceptional seeds.

## DG-018-C — record-index repetition and later-weight ordering (PROVED CONDITIONALLY)

On an unbounded \(m\in E\) orbit, every tail minimum \(a_j\) is odd (an even one would immediately halve below itself). As \(a_{j+1}\) is the minimum of the orbit after \(t_j\),

\[
a_{j+1}\le T(a_j)=\frac{3a_j+1}{2},
\qquad a_j+1\le(3/2)^j(m+1).
\]

Equality can occur in the first bound when the record gap is one: **do not** assert universal strictness. If length-\(h\) parity factors starting at actual record times \(t_i<t_j\) agree, parity-cylinder uniqueness yields \(2^h\mid(a_j-a_i)\). As records strictly increase,

\[
2^h\le a_j-a_i<a_j+1\le(3/2)^j(m+1).
\]

For DG017's true-orbit weight \(W_m(t)=(3/2)^{s_t}[(m+2)/(2(m+1))]^{t-s_t}\), the larger state is now provably at the *later* index \(t_j\), so

\[
\boxed{W_m(t_j)\ge(a_j+1)/(m+1)\ge1+2^h/(m+1).}
\]

This **later-only** bound is sound for these ordered record-start factors, but is false at unrestricted time pairs (finite witness \(3\to5\to8\to4\)). Defining \(P_m^{\rm rec}(h)\) as the number of different length-\(h\) parity factors at all actual record starts and \(\mu=\log_2(3/2)\), the preceding record-*index* inequality proves, for \(h\ge\log_2(m+1)\),

\[
P_m^{\rm rec}(h)\ge
1+\left\lfloor\frac{h-\log_2(m+1)}{\mu}\right\rfloor.
\]

This is stronger in *index type* than DG016's elapsed-time factor bound and independent of arbitrarily long gaps. But it does not force a forbidden early repetition and cannot eliminate high-complexity hypothetical E orbits.

## DG-018-D — failed global attacks, preperiodic residual, novelty

**No unrestricted high-complexity obstruction.** The side-root construction is unconditional **for a rising \(1^s0^r\) bridge** but requires that bridge shape. Strict finite Champernowne mountain prefixes have exact side roots at *each* completed bridge (not just none), while unrestricted nonmountain finite bridges such as \(27\to41\to62\to31\) are outside this specific lemma. Neither condition implies a fixed ordinary positive E orbit with infinitely many such bridges, or universal descent.

**No density contradiction:** \(R_m(X)+M_m(X)\) can be \(O(\log X)\) in abstract feasible record-growth models. Nothing here proves positive-frequency mountains in genuine E, disjoint abundance of other inverse-root families, or a lower bound incompatible with \(o(X)\). DG010's general two-gap conditional count remains useful when A's special path is absent.

**Bounded/preperiodic E still open.** A hypothetical positive nontrivial cycle with parity length \(L\), \(s\) odds and affine numerator \(Q>0\) obeys \((2^L-3^s)y=Q\), \(2^L>3^s\), and divisibility \((2^L-3^s)\mid Q\). Its first outside entry \(y\) must be \(2\bmod3\). If \(m=\min E\) enters after \(t\ge1\) steps with entry-prefix odd count \(u\) and numerator \(q\), then \(m=(2^t y-q)/3^u>0\), all pre-entry states are at least \(m\), \(m\le c=\min C\), and \(y\ge T(m)\) (strict if first entry is after the first step). This is no demonstrated all-words contradiction. Do **not** identify \(m\), \(c\), and a possible precursor \((2c-1)/3\). The finite shadow \(15\to23\to35\to53\to80\to40\to20\to10\) refutes using least-E visit floors outside an actual least E member.
