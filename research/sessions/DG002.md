# DG002 — positive-integer realisation obstruction (2026-10-15)

**Executed.** Scope: shortened Collatz map on positive integers. Elementary analytic proofs and exact finite checks; **not Lean-certified, not a proof that E is empty, and not a claim of mathematical novelty**. Continuation: [DG003](../NEXT_SESSION_DG003.md).

## 0. Question and inherited foundations

For \(T(n)=n/2\) on even \(n\) and \(T(n)=(3n+1)/2\) on odd \(n\), put
\[
E=\{n\ge2:\ T^j(n)\ge n\text{ for every }j\ge1\}.
\]
DG001 proved that each finite binary parity word \(w=(\varepsilon_0,\ldots,\varepsilon_{m-1})\) has unique residue \(r(w)\pmod {2^m}\), and that for numbers with that prefix
\[
T^m(x)=\frac{Ax+q}{D},\qquad
A=3^{s},\ D=2^m,\ s=\sum\varepsilon_i,\ q=q_w\ge0.
\]
The affine formula is only valid along the specified parity branch. At every step inside the word, DG001's inequalities \(3^{s_j}x+q_j\ge 2^jx\) are necessary and sufficient for no descent.

**Aim tested:** determine when compatible arbitrarily long *surviving* parity prefixes are actually realised by one fixed positive integer, especially without pretending that a 2-adic itinerary is automatically a positive integer.

## 1. DG-002-A: exact repeated-block divisibility (PROVED)

**Theorem.** Let \(w\) be any length-\(m\ge1\) parity word with data \((A,D,q)\) as above. If an integer \(x>0\) begins with \(w^t\) (the block repeated \(t\ge1\) times), then
\[
\boxed{D^t\mid (D-A)x-q,\quad D=2^m.}\tag{1}
\]
If \((D-A)x-q\ne0\), its number of consecutive complete initial \(w\)-blocks is at most
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{v_2(|(D-A)x-q|)}m\right\rfloor.\tag{2}
\]
For an **expanding** word \(A>D\), necessarily \(q>0\), and
\[
\boxed{D^t\le (A-D)x+q,\qquad x\ge \frac{D^t-q}{A-D}.}\tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** At block boundaries put \(x_i=T^{im}(x)\). The specified repetitions imply \(Dx_{i+1}=Ax_i+q\) for \(0\le i<t\). A direct induction gives
\[
D^t x_t=A^t x+q\sum_{h=0}^{t-1}A^{t-1-h}D^h.
\]
Multiply by \(D-A\) and use \((D-A)\sum A^{t-1-h}D^h=D^t-A^t\). Rearranging,
\[
A^t((D-A)x-q)=D^t((D-A)x_t-q).\tag{4}
\]
Since \(A\) is odd, \(\gcd(A^t,D^t)=1\), so (1). For nonzero difference, divisibility gives (2). If \(A>D\), then \(q>0\) (at least one odd step, because \(A>1\)) and \((D-A)x-q<0\) for positive \(x\). Its positive absolute value is \((A-D)x+q\), at least \(D^t\), giving (3). QED.

**Important scope.** Equation (1) is a necessary arithmetic condition for the repeated itinerary, not a claim that a given finite word automatically repeats for all integers. In fact the unique congruence root of (1), modulo \(D^t\), is precisely the cylinder for \(w^t\): by DG001, \(w^t\) has one residue and its members satisfy (1); \(D-A\) is odd, hence invertible modulo \(D^t\).

## 2. DG-002-B: periodic parity is an actual integer cycle (PROVED)

**Theorem.** If a *positive integer* \(x\) has parity itinerary \(w^\infty\) for some nonempty finite word \(w\), then
\[
\boxed{x=\frac{q}{D-A}\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\quad\text{and}\quad T^m(x)=x.}\tag{5}
\]
Conversely, if \(q/(D-A)\) is a positive integer, that integer indeed follows \(w^\infty\). In particular **no expanding word \(A>D\) is the infinite repeating parity itinerary of any positive integer**. If a positive-integer orbit has an *eventually* periodic parity itinerary, its numeric orbit is eventually periodic.

**Proof.** Applying (1) for every \(t\) means the fixed integer \((D-A)x-q\) is divisible by arbitrarily large powers \(2^{mt}\), hence is zero. Since \(x>0\), (5) forces \(D>A\) and \(q>0\). For the converse, \(Ac+q=Dc\) at \(c=q/(D-A)>0\). In particular \(Ac+q\equiv0\pmod D\); DG001 says this congruence selects precisely the residue of \(w\), because the residue of any actual \(w\)-prefix also satisfies it and \(A\) is invertible modulo \(D\). Thus \(c\) follows the first block \(w\) and \(T^m(c)=c\); iteration repeats the block. For an eventually periodic itinerary apply the argument to the positive integer reached when its periodic tail starts. QED.

This does **not** rule out unproved nontrivial positive Collatz cycles: a contracting word with an integer fixed point would describe one. It only rules out indefinitely repeating *expanding* blocks and unrealised rational fixed points. No cycle-only result has been promoted to universal descent.

## 3. DG-002-C: sharp mixed-parity temporary survivors (PROVED)

For \(w=110\) (odd, odd, even), the block map is
\[
T^3(x)=\frac{9x+5}{8},\qquad (A,D,q)=(9,8,5).
\]
Therefore any positive integer with prefix \((110)^t\) obeys
\[
8^t\mid x+5,\qquad x\ge 8^t-5.\tag{6}
\]
The lower bound is attained for **every** \(t\ge1\) by \(n_t=8^t-5\). Indeed \(n_t\equiv-5\pmod{8^t}\), as does the *negative* integral period-3 orbit
\[
-5\ \longrightarrow\ -7\ \longrightarrow\ -10\ \longrightarrow\ -5,
\]
with parity word \(110\). By the DG001 parity-cylinder bijection, \(n_t\) has prefix \((110)^t\). More explicitly, at block boundaries
\[
T^{3j}(n_t)=9^j8^{t-j}-5\quad(0\le j\le t).\tag{7}
\]
For \(j<t\) the boundary value is odd and positive; the next two odd steps strictly increase and the third step returns to the next block boundary. Because \((9/8)^j\ge1\), each block boundary and hence **all** the intervening iterates through step \(3t\) are at least \(n_t\) (strict after time 0). Finally \(T^{3t}(n_t)=9^t-5\) is even, whereas another \(110\) block would start odd. Thus exactly \(t\) consecutive \(110\) blocks occur at the start, and the first \(3t\) steps do not descend. The limiting periodic parity word \((110)^\infty\) belongs to the 2-adic value \(-5\), **not to any positive integer**.

**Killed implication:** arbitrarily long repetitions of a mixed parity word, and arbitrarily long survival even with a *strictly expanding* affine multiplier at every prefix, do not produce one positive permanent survivor. The starting integers \(n_t\) vary and grow exponentially.

## 4. DG-002-D: shifted block growth excludes some nonperiodic 2-adic paths (PROVED)

**Lemma (unconditional orbit envelope).** For every integer \(n\ge1\) and \(J\ge0\),
\[
T^J(n)+1\le(3/2)^J(n+1).\tag{8}
\]
Each step satisfies \(T(y)+1\le\frac32(y+1)\): equality on odd \(y\), inequality on even \(y\ge1\). Iterate.

If the itinerary of a fixed positive integer \(n\) contains an expanding block \(w\) repeated \(t\) times **starting at position \(J\)**, apply (3) to \(x=T^J(n)\) and then (8):
\[
\boxed{D^t\le (A-D)\left((3/2)^J(n+1)-1\right)+q.}\tag{9}
\]
In particular an occurrence of \((110)^t\) beginning at position \(J\) requires
\[
\boxed{8^t\le(3/2)^J(n+1)+4.}\tag{10}
\]
Thus no fixed positive-integer orbit can exhibit \(110\)-block run lengths \(t_i\) at positions \(J_i\to\infty\) with, for example, \(t_i\ge J_i+1\). More generally, any schedule violating (9) infinitely often is impossible for positive integer data. This restriction is not confined to eventually periodic words.

**Explicit nonperiodic countermodel to finite-to-infinite realisation.**
Build an infinite parity word \(W\) inductively. Start at \(J_1=0\); at position \(J_i\), append \((110)^{t_i}\) with \(t_i=J_i+1\), then append a single odd symbol \(1\). Set
\[
J_{i+1}=J_i+3t_i+1=4J_i+4.
\]
The first run lengths are \(1,5,21,85,\ldots\); the separator bits prevent ultimate periodicity. Every nonempty prefix \(u\) has \(3^{s(u)}>2^{|u|}\): each prefix of a \(110\) block has multiplier ratio \(3/2,9/4,9/8>1\) and each separator \(1\) multiplies by \(3/2\). Hence **every finite prefix has \(M(u)=+\infty\)** by DG001: the whole positive tail of its parity residue class survives every step through that prefix.

The residues for successive finite prefixes are compatible, so they specify a 2-adic integer, and positive integer representatives exist at each finite depth. But any *fixed* positive integer \(n\) with itinerary \(W\) would satisfy
\[
8^{J_i+1}\le(3/2)^{J_i}(n+1)+4\quad\text{for every }i,
\]
contradicting \(J_i\to\infty\), since the left side grows exponentially faster than the right. Therefore this coherent, non-eventually-periodic, universally finite-surviving 2-adic parity path is **not realised by a positive integer**.

**Exact failure located:** compatibility of every residue class, nonempty infinite finite-survival cylinders and strict supercriticality of *all* their affine prefix multipliers still do not prove an ordinary positive integer survives forever. An additional fixed-integer realisation argument is indispensable.

## 5. DG-002-E: necessary odd frequency for a genuine exception (PROVED)

Let \(n\in E\), \(x_j=T^j(n)\ge n\), and \(s_k\) count the odd step inputs among \(x_0,\dots,x_{k-1}\). For odd \(x_j\ge n\),
\[
\frac{x_{j+1}}{x_j}=\frac{3+1/x_j}{2}\le \frac{3+1/n}{2};
\]
for even \(x_j\), the ratio is \(1/2\). Multiplying and using \(x_k\ge n\),
\[
1\le\frac{x_k}{n}\le \frac{(3+1/n)^{s_k}}{2^k},
\]
so for **every** \(k\ge1\)
\[
\boxed{s_k\ge\left\lceil\frac{k\log2}{\log(3+1/n)}\right\rceil>\frac k2\ \text{(as a real lower threshold)}.}\tag{11}
\]
More precisely \(s_k>k/2\), because \(3+1/n<4\) for \(n\ge2\). In particular E-members have odd strict majority along every initial parity prefix, and no even \(n\) belongs to E. This restricts genuine exceptional integer orbits at **all** horizons, not just a chosen depth; it is necessary, not sufficient. As \(n\to\infty\), the threshold approaches \(\log 2/\log3\). This elementary drift condition does not eliminate all parity paths and does not strengthen the published Terras–Everett density-zero conclusion to emptiness.

## 6. Counterexamples, attempted stronger inferences, and status

- **False:** “If every finite truncation along one coherent 2-adic path survives, it determines one positive permanent survivor.” Counterexample: the explicit nonperiodic \(W\) in §4; all its finite-prefix M-values are infinite, yet no positive integer realises it. The periodic \(110\) path supplies a simpler \(-5\) example.
- **False:** “A long mixed/expanding block must force descent within a uniform number of steps.” The positive integers \(8^t-5\) survive \(3t\) steps following \((110)^t\).
- **False:** “M(w)=infinity for a prefix proves that its actual positive representatives lie in E.” For example \(3\) begins \(11\) (both prefix homogeneous multipliers exceed powers of 2) but \(3\to5\to8\to4\to2<3\).
- **Not proved:** “A nonperiodic path satisfying (11) cannot be realised by a positive integer.” The construction of §4 kills one subclass only, not all possibilities. Establishing a universal such implication would require new mathematics.
- **Not proved:** “An eventual periodic parity path is incompatible with E.” §2 reduces a *positive* eventually periodic itinerary to an actual numeric cycle. Excluding all nontrivial positive cycles remains unproved, and in any case does not exclude hypothetical nonperiodic E-trajectories.
- **Not proved:** “Density-zero or arbitrarily strong finite-prefix restrictions force E empty.” A sparse exceptional set is not excluded on these premises.

## 8. Net effect on E and exact next barrier

**Positive restrictions established:** an E-member must obey the infinite family of odd-prefix drift inequalities (11); any positive orbit (including a hypothetical E-member) must obey the exact repetition and shifted-growth constraints (1)–(3), (9); an eventually periodic parity tail must be a genuine numeric cycle. This excludes specific impossible infinite 2-adic paths, including explicit *nonperiodic* paths. It does **not** exclude a new named numerical congruence class of E beyond finite-depth tests, nor prove E empty.

**Barrier:** for a hypothetical fixed \(n\in E\), growth of \(T^J(n)\) may allow repetitions starting sufficiently late, and a nonperiodic word with no forbidden repetitions can still satisfy every currently known prefix condition. Need a non-circular positive-integer constraint powerful enough to exclude actual individual permanent survivors, not just paths which have no positive-integer realisation anyway.
