## 1. DG-016-A: global factor-complexity bound (PROVED, genuine unbounded orbit)

Put \(\mu=\log_2(3/2)=0.5849625007\ldots\). For one ordinary positive seed \(n\), write \(x_t=T^t(n)\), let \(\omega_t=x_t\bmod2\), and define \(P_n(h)\) as the number of distinct length-\(h\) factors of the **infinite** binary word \(\omega_0\omega_1\cdots\).

**Theorem.** If the positive orbit of \(n\) is not eventually periodic (equivalently, it is unbounded), then for every integer \(h\ge1\) with \(h\ge H_n:=\log_2(n+1)\),

\[
 P_n(h)\ \ge\ 1+\left\lfloor\frac{h-H_n}{\mu}\right\rfloor.                 \tag{1}
\]

In particular, \(\liminf_{h\to\infty}P_n(h)/h\ge1/\mu=1.709511291\ldots\). Therefore an unbounded positive orbit cannot have a parity word satisfying \(P_n(h)\le Ch+B\) for infinitely many arbitrarily large \(h\), where \(C<1/\mu\) and \(B\) is fixed. This excludes *all Sturmian parity words* (\(P(h)=h+1\)), not just named slopes, on **unbounded ordinary positive** Collatz orbits. It says nothing about whether a Sturmian infinite word is realised by a nonpositive or 2-adic seed.

**Proof.** For every positive \(x\), \(T(x)+1\le\frac32(x+1)\): this is equality for odd \(x\), strict for even \(x\). Hence \(x_t+1\le(3/2)^t(n+1)\) for every \(t\ge0\). By the exact shortened-Collatz parity-cylinder bijection, two positive integers producing the same first \(h\) parity symbols are congruent modulo \(2^h\).

Suppose the length-\(h\) factors at starts \(0\le p<q\) coincide. The states \(x_p,x_q\) are distinct: on a deterministic map, one repeated state forces eventual periodicity. Thus \(|x_q-x_p|\ge2^h\) by divisibility, but
\[
|x_q-x_p|<\max(x_p+1,x_q+1)\le(3/2)^q(n+1).
\]
Consequently every such repetition obeys the **exact collision inequality**
\[
 2^h<(3/2)^q(n+1),\quad\text{i.e.}\quad 2^{h+q}<3^q(n+1).       \tag{2}
\]
If \(q\le\lfloor(h-H_n)/\mu\rfloor\), (2) is impossible. Therefore length-\(h\) factors beginning at indices \(0,\ldots,\lfloor(h-H_n)/\mu\rfloor\) are pairwise distinct, proving (1). \(\square\)

**Effect on E.** Every hypothetical unbounded orbit starting at any \(m\in E\) must satisfy (1), with no mountain or record-word hypothesis. This is a real *positive-integer* necessary condition, not just symbolic shadow arithmetic. However high complexity is compatible with a putative E orbit, and a lower bound on **factors of one itinerary** is not a lower bound on **distinct E integers up to X**. It cannot contradict Terras–Everett's \(E=o(X)\).

**Important scope boundary:** Eventually periodic bounded orbits (including ordinary orbits entering \(1\leftrightarrow2\)) do not satisfy the injectivity premise, so (1) must not be applied to them. Finite traces ending at a cycle likewise provide no unbounded-orbit witness.

## 2. DG-016-B: strengthened mountain factor-complexity (PROVED, conditional on exact infinite mountain itinerary)

Keep the DG010–DG015 strict mountain blocks \(w_r=1^{s(r)}0^r\), for \(r\ge5,\ r\equiv5\bmod6\), and \(d(r)=s(r)+r\ge14\). Let \(r_0,r_1,\ldots\) be an **infinite prescribed mountain-parameter sequence actually followed by one ordinary positive integer** \(n=a_0\). Its block starts \(a_j\) satisfy
\[
a_{j+1}=\lambda_{r_j}a_j+\eta_{r_j},\quad
1<\lambda_{r_j}<3/2,\quad0<\eta_{r_j}<3/2.
\]
Thus (sharpening the \(a_q+1<2^q(n+1)\) bound in DG015)
\[
a_{j+1}+3<\tfrac32(a_j+3),\qquad
a_q+3<(3/2)^q(n+3)\quad(q\ge1).                        \tag{3}
\]

If identical parameter factors of length \(h\) start at \(p<q\), their common **binary** length is
\(L=\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}d(r_{p+i})\ge14h\). DG015's cylinder congruence gives \(2^L\mid(a_q-a_p)\); strict mountain growth gives \(a_q>a_p\). Combining (3),
\[
2^L\le a_q-a_p<a_q+3<(3/2)^q(n+3),                    \tag{4}
\]
so every such repeated factor must satisfy
\[
q>\frac{L-\log_2(n+3)}{\mu}\ \ge\
\frac{14h-\log_2(n+3)}{\mu}.                           \tag{5}
\]

Write \(P_r(h)\) for the length-\(h\) factor complexity of the parameter word. For every \(h\) with \(14h\ge\log_2(n+3)\),
\[
P_r(h)\ge1+\left\lfloor\frac{14h-\log_2(n+3)}{\mu}\right\rfloor,\qquad
\liminf_{h\to\infty}P_r(h)/h\ge\frac{14}{\mu}
=23.933158079\ldots.                                 \tag{6}
\]
More sharply, for a particular repeated pair the *actual* \(L=\sum d(r)\) must be used in (5); the 14 is only a universal minimum. If all permitted parameters lie in a class with \(d(r)\ge d_*>14\), replace 14 by \(d_*\).

**Broad nonrealisation corollary.** Any infinite parameter itinerary with \(P_r(h)\le Ch+B\) for arbitrarily large \(h\), where \(C<14/\mu\) and \(B\) is fixed, cannot be followed by a single ordinary positive integer. This includes periodic/eventually periodic, Sturmian, quasi-Sturmian (\(h+O(1)\)), Arnoux–Rauzy ternary (\(2h+1\)), and any individually established automatic/substitutive sequence whose actual factor-complexity constant is below the threshold. We do **not** assert the bound for every automatic/substitutive sequence or every linearly recurrent word without an explicit complexity estimate.

**Proof of (6).** A repetition between any two start indices \(0\le p<q\le\lfloor(14h-\log_2(n+3))/\mu\rfloor\) contradicts (5). All factors starting within that index range are therefore distinct. \(\square\)

This automatically disposes of many low-complexity mountain schedules beyond the two named Thue adversaries of DG015. It still assumes the *whole* infinite trajectory is partitioned into the specified strict mountains, which is not known for arbitrary E.

## 3. DG-016-C: exact high-complexity finite-shadow adversary (PROVED, not an E orbit)

Let \(c\) be the ternary Champernowne word obtained by concatenating, for each \(k=1,2,\ldots\), all \(3^k\) ternary strings of length \(k\) in lexicographic order. Every ternary length-\(h\) word appears verbatim in the length-\(h\) stage, so its factor complexity is **exactly** \(P_c(h)=3^h\) for all \(h\). Map symbols \(0,1,2\) to mountain parameters \(5,11,17\).

Every *finite* prefix of this mountain sequence has infinitely many positive integer strict-bridge realisations by the existing DG010/DG011 finite CRT construction. Its exponential factor complexity evades the **asymptotic linear** obstruction (6): \(3^h>(14/\mu)h-O(1)\) at large \(h\). This is an explicit counterexample to claiming the factor-complexity inequality by itself excludes **every finite-alphabet symbolic mountain schedule**. It is NOT an example of a single positive integer realising the entire infinite word; that existence question remains open for this adversary.

Likewise, the general parity bound (1) does not imply an exceptional orbit must be periodic or must use mountains. It excludes genuinely low-complexity words only.

## 4. DG-016-D: actual least-E and bounded-case audit (no global exclusion)

The DG015 least-E floor holds: if \(m=\min E\), \(m\) is odd and any visit \(y=T^t(m)\equiv2\bmod3\) with \(t\ge1\) obeys \(y\ge T(m)\). The proposed strengthening that *mere long finite survival* implies the same floor is FALSE. Exact counterexample:
\[
15\to23\to35\to53\to80\to40\to20\to10.
\]
The starting value 15 survives without descent through the visit \(y=20\equiv2\bmod3\) at \(t=6\), yet \(20<T(15)=23\). It descends at \(t=7\); the shorter odd predecessor \((2\cdot20-1)/3=13\) is not an infinite exceptional integer. The least-E floor needs an actual infinite E member, not an arbitrary finite positive shadow.

For a hypothetical least 3-divisible E seed entering a nontrivial cycle, the first cycle value remains \(2\bmod3\) and \(\ge T(m)\) (strict if first entry is after its first step). The cycle's unknown full parity itinerary and its preperiodic branch provide no new prohibitive inequality here. Bounded/preperiodic E therefore remains OPEN. The sharp DG011 \(\ge7\)-odd and \(\ge11\)-step shield for expanding inverses of \(7\bmod9\) endpoints, and DG010's two-gap root collision rule, remain intact: (1) produces no new off-spine inverse root at all.
